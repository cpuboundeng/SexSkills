# CLOTHING PEEKS (Upskirt, Down-Blouse, See-Through, Sideboob)

## Overview

Clothing peeks are the erotic charge of the *partial reveal* — skin, underwear, or a body's shape glimpsed *through or around* clothes rather than fully exposed. They sit at the exact center of the gaze: the watcher catches a sliver, the wearer (sometimes) invites or withholds it, and the imagination does the rest. This is the softest, most everyday register of exhibitionism and voyeurism — no nudity required, which is precisely its power.

Where full exposure resolves the tension, a peek *withholds* it. The brain fills the gap: a hemline that *might* ride up, a neckline that *almost* shows, a fabric that *maybe* is transparent in the right light. Plausible deniability is the engine — the glance could be an accident, the outfit could be innocent, and both parties get to pretend otherwise.

**Key distinction:** These are *gaze* kinks, not *touch* kinks. Once the fabric comes off or a hand reaches in, the scene has moved past the peek into `foreplay`, `breast-play`, or `sex`. The peek's charge lives in the *looking* — before anything is touched.

## The Four Archetypes

| Archetype | What's revealed | The mechanism | Read |
|-----------|-----------------|---------------|------|
| **Upskirt** | Thighs, underwear, the hint beyond | A skirt riding up; a staircase, a crossed leg, a breeze, a dropped object, a seated angle | The most "stolen" — often the watcher's deliberate line of sight |
| **Down-blouse** | Cleavage, the upper breast, a bra | A neckline that gapes when the wearer leans, bends, or reaches | The glance from above; proximity + angle |
| **See-through** | The whole silhouette — nipple, panty line, bare skin | Sheer fabric, a wet shirt, a backlit dress, thin white material | The most deliberate; the wearer usually *knows* the fabric shows |
| **Sideboob** | The curve of the breast around an open side | An armhole, a halter, a wrap dress, a shirt unbuttoned at the side | The sliver that implies the whole; the most "accidental-seeming" of breast reveals |

**Cousins in the same family:** cleavage/underboob (the low neckline, the crop top), the panty shot (a glimpse of underwear — upskirt's sibling), the wardrobe malfunction (a strap slipping, a button popping, a towel gaping), the wet t-shirt (see-through's playful cousin), and the backlit/translucent silhouette (the figure outlined through fabric).

## The Peek Beat

The core unit of every clothing-peek scene. It has a rhythm:

1. **The setup** — the angle, the fabric, the movement (leaning, bending, crossing legs, reaching up) that *creates* the potential.
2. **The glimpse** — the split-second of skin or fabric-shape. Half a second, then it's gone.
3. **The aftermath** — the watcher's reaction (the held breath, the look away, the look *back*), and the question: did the wearer notice? Did they *mean* it?

Write the glimpse short. The power is in the near-miss — the thing *almost* seen, the angle that *almost* lines up. A peek that lingers too long stops being a peek and becomes a view; different register, different heat.

## Both Perspectives

### The watcher
- **The stolen glance** — looking while pretending not to; the furtive, the quick, the guilty.
- **The held breath** — the stillness of the caught gaze; the body tense with the effort of not being obvious.
- **The line of sight** — the watcher positions for the angle: a lower seat, a step down, a glance over a shoulder. The gaze as action (see `voyeurism.md`).
- **The imagination** — filling in what the glimpse only hinted at. The peek is a *doorway*, not a destination.

### The wearer (when they know)
- **The accidental exposure** — genuinely unaware; the charge is the watcher's alone.
- **The invitation** — the deliberate outfit (the short skirt, the low top, the sheer blouse), chosen *to be seen*.
- **The performance** — adjusting, bending, crossing, leaning — "accidentally" giving the view while feigning ignorance.
- **The power** — the wearer controls the glimpse; they can grant it or take it away, and both the granting and the withholding are the kink.

The richest scenes give the wearer *some* awareness — even if unspoken. A peek where both sides know (and pretend not to) is hotter than a pure one-way steal, because it becomes a silent negotiation.

## Consent & Frame

Clothing peeks sit on the same consent gradient as all voyeurism/exhibitionism (see `voyeurism.md`):

- **Consensual / invited** — the wearer dresses and performs for the gaze; the watcher's look is wanted. Cleanest and often hottest.
- **Ambiguous** — the wearer doesn't know. Write as the watcher's private transgression (with an inner war, a consequence, or guilt) — or give it the classic **turn**: the watcher is caught, and the wearer *invites them in* (transgression → discovery → invitation → participation).
- **The non-consensual creep** — an unframed, predatory stare (e.g., deliberately angling for an upskirt of someone who clearly doesn't want it) reads as violation, not kink. Frame it, name it, or give it consequences.

**Guideline:** the peek is hottest when the *looking* is earned or welcomed — a shared secret rather than a theft.

## Where Each Peek Goes Next

The peek is a *beginning*. Map its handoff:

- **Upskirt / panty shot** → `foreplay` (the hand finding the thigh), `cunnilingus` (the skirt pushed up), or `exhibitionism-voyeurism` (the flash).
- **Down-blouse / cleavage** → `breast-play` (the neckline lowered, the breast revealed and touched), `kissing` (the mouth at the collarbone).
- **See-through / wet t-shirt** → `foreplay` (the fabric peeled away), `exhibitionism-voyeurism` (the public exposure).
- **Sideboob** → `breast-play` (the curve bared and worshipped) — or, if it stays a *look*, it stays here.

## Writing Tips

- **Keep the glimpse brief.** The peek's power is the near-miss. Half a sentence of skin, then the fabric falls back into place.
- **Write the watcher's body.** The held breath, the racing pulse, the effort not to stare — the watcher has a body too (see `voyeurism.md`).
- **Use deniability.** "It was an accident. It kept happening." The will-they/won't-they of *whether it was meant* is the tease — this links directly to the `seduction` skill's push-pull.
- **Vary the angle.** A fixed stare is monotonous. Change the line of sight, the risk of being caught, the distance.
- **The turn.** The caught-watcher beat — the wearer meeting their eye and *letting them look* — is the classic payoff. Don't skip it unless the distance is the whole point.
- **Hand it off.** The peek wants to become something. Route it forward (see "Where Each Peek Goes Next") before it wears thin.

## Scene Structure Example

1. **The Setup** — The angle appears: a low-cut blouse, a short skirt, a breeze. The watcher notices the *potential* before anything shows.

2. **The Glimpse** — She leans forward and the neckline gapes — a sliver, a shadow of lace, then gone. He forgets to breathe.

3. **The Inner War** — He shouldn't look. He looks. The skirt rides a little higher when she uncrosses her legs; the fabric clings where it shouldn't.

4. **The Almost** — She reaches up to a high shelf and the hem lifts — just shy of showing everything. The angle is *perfect*. It doesn't happen. It's the best part.

5. **The Discovery / Turn** — She catches him looking and holds his gaze for a beat too long — then doesn't pull the hem down. "See something you like?" A smile. An invitation.

6. **The Handoff** — The deniability collapses (see `seduction`), the first touch lands (see `foreplay`), or the fabric finally comes away (see `breast-play`).

## Quick Reference: Scene Arc

1. **Setup** — the angle, the potential
2. **Glimpse** — the half-second of skin
3. **Inner war** — guilt vs. looking again
4. **Almost** — the near-miss that withholds
5. **Turn** — the caught gaze, the invitation
6. **Handoff** — into touch, flash, or reveal
