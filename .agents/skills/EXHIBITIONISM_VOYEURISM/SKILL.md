# EXHIBITIONISM & VOYEURISM

## Overview

Exhibitionism and voyeurism are two sides of one coin: the **gaze**. Exhibitionism is arousal from *being seen*; voyeurism is arousal from *seeing*. Most good scenes lean on both — a watcher and a watched, each amplifying the other — and the two kinks are so tightly paired that they belong under one roof.

The distinction is **direction**:

| Kink | The thrill | The gaze |
|------|-----------|----------|
| **Exhibitionism** | Being seen, exposure, risk of discovery | Directed *at* the self |
| **Voyeurism** | Seeing, access to a private moment | Directed *outward* |

Within exhibitionism there's a further split between the **non-sexual** (nudism/naturism — comfort, body acceptance) and the **erotic** (exhibitionism proper — the thrill of being seen, flashing, public sex). Keeping these apart is the single most important accuracy point: a nudist is not an exhibitionist.

**Key framing for fiction:** Both kinks sit on a **consent gradient**. Consensual watching/being-watched (invited shows, performances, "watch me") is the cleanest and often the hottest. Non-consensual flashing or peeping is a transgression — write it *with intent* (as the character's taboo, with consequences) or give it a **turn** (the watcher discovered and invited in). The frame is what keeps the kink erotic rather than alienating.

## The Spectrum

### Exhibitionism (being seen)
- **Soft** — subtle, barely noticeable exposure; the "maybe someone saw" thrill.
- **Moderate** — deliberate, controlled exposure to a few witnesses.
- **Hard / risk** — full exposure or public sex where discovery is the point.
- **Non-sexual** — nudism/naturism: comfort, community, body acceptance (not erotic).

### Voyeurism (watching)
- **Accidental** — stumbling on a view; "I can't look away."
- **Deliberate / peeping** — seeking the view; darker, intent-driven.
- **Consensual / invited** — the watched knows and performs for the gaze.
- **The discovered watcher** — the gaze made visible; often the scene's turn.
- **Clothing peeks** — the partial reveal: upskirt, down-blouse, see-through, sideboob. Skin glimpsed *through or around* clothes; no nudity required (see `references/clothing_peeks.md`).

See the references for the deep treatment of each direction.

## The Watcher/Watched Dynamic

The erotic engine is the *loop* between the two:
- The watched **performs** for the gaze — arching more, moving slower, playing to the audience.
- The watcher **projects** — imagining being touched the way they're watching someone be touched.
- The **secret** — each holds a knowledge the other may not share, until the discovery.

In fiction this loop is doubly potent: the reader is *already* a voyeur, so a voyeurism scene can implicate the reader's own gaze directly.

## Consent & Safety

- **Consensual pair** — invitation, a club, an exhibitionist partner, an agreed scenario. The gaze is shared.
- **Ambiguous** — the watched doesn't know. Write as transgression (with consequence) or as fantasy with a consensual frame.
- **The turn** — transgression → discovery → invitation → participation. A classic arc.
- **Legal note** — indecent exposure and public sex laws vary by jurisdiction; car sex in a parked car with a reasonable expectation of privacy is often treated differently from exposure in a visible public space.

## Scene Structure Skeleton

1. **The setup** — the moment, the location, the degree of exposure or the discovery of a view.
2. **The build** — anticipation rises; risk or longing mounts.
3. **The act** — the exposure (being seen) or the watch (seeing).
4. **The reaction** — the witness responds, or the watcher's inner war peaks.
5. **The turn** — discovery, invitation, or the near-miss that resolves the scene.
6. **The aftermath** — the euphoria, regret, giddy rush, or the lingering ache of distance.
