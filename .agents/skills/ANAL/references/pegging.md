## Why It Works — The Psychology

- **Role reversal.** The person who is usually penetrated gets to *do* the penetrating; the person who usually does gets to *receive*. Both partners get to live inside a role they rarely occupy. This is the core thrill, and it can be framed as liberation, play, power, or intimacy.
- **Masculinity under negotiation.** For a cis man, being penetrated often rubs against everything he was taught about being "the one in charge." That tension — wanting it anyway, surrendering to it, feeling more himself *because* of it — is rich material. Pegging scenes live here.
- **Submission is a choice, not a condition.** Receiving does not automatically make a man submissive, and wearing the strap-on does not automatically make a woman dominant. Pegging can be tender and mutual, purely pleasurable, or explicitly D/s. Write the *frame the characters chose*, not a stereotype.
- **Prostate pleasure as discovery.** For many men, pegging is the first reliable route to prostate orgasms — deep, full-body, and different from penile ones. The "male G-spot" is the physical reward inside the psychological one.
- **Trust and vulnerability.** Being penetrated somewhere you can't see, by someone who is learning a new motion, requires enormous trust. The giver is also vulnerable — new equipment, new choreography, the fear of "doing it wrong." Both partners are beginners in their own way, and that shared clumsiness can be the most human, intimate thing on the page.

## Power Dynamics

Pegging spans the full spectrum — don't default to "woman dominates man":

- **Femdom / role reversal** — the classic read: she drives, commands, uses him; he submits. Signature lines: "You're going to take all of me."
- **Tender / service** — she penetrates *for* him, to give him the prostate orgasm he's never had, gentleness and care. "Tell me if it's too much."
- **Mutual / playful** — two people trying something new, laughing, discovering. The clumsiness is the point.
- **Submission of the giver** — rarer and interesting: she wears the strap-on *in service* to a dominant partner who orders her to fuck him. The harness doesn't decide who's in charge.
- **Queer / feminist** — pegging as gender play, as refusing the "penetrator = man" script, as two people exploring the full range of their bodies.

2. **Skipping the prep.** Lube, warm-up, relaxation — the anal basics still apply. A pegging scene without them reads as careless (or painful).

6. **Heteronormative framing.** Pegging isn't only cis-man/woman, and it isn't "gay" because a man is penetrated. Keep it about *these two people*.
