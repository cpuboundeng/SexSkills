
# FACESITTING

## Overview

Facesitting is oral sex performed with the receiver seated or kneeling over the giver's face, so the giver is beneath, mouth working upward, while the receiver controls depth, pressure, and pace from above. It is the act most strongly associated with a dominance frame — the receiver literally sits on the source of their pleasure — but it spans a wide tonal range, from worshipful service to playful smothering to tender, unhurried closeness.

Its appeal is control and reversal. The conventional "oral" dynamic (giver kneeling before the receiver) is inverted: now the receiver is *above*, and the giver's whole world is reduced to breath, taste, and the weight of a partner's body. For the receiver, the pleasure is doubled — the physical sensation of the mouth, plus the psychological thrill of being positioned, held, and surrendered to. For the giver, the appeal is the devotion itself: being *used* as the instrument of someone's pleasure, face pressed between thighs, asked for nothing in return.

## The Psychology

- **Control from above** — the receiver sets the pace, the pressure, and when to ease off. They can grind, hover, or hold the giver still.
- **Service from below** — the giver's pleasure is secondary; their reward is giving. This is a submissive or worshipful posture.
- **Smothering / breath play** — the receiver's weight and thighs can restrict the giver's air. Even *implied* breathlessness raises intensity (safety note: always leave a way to tap out).
- **Reversal** — the person usually "served" can suddenly be the one serving; a power dynamic flipped for a scene.
- **Enclosure** — the giver is surrounded by the receiver's body: thighs, hips, scent, heat, the press of hands. Total sensory immersion.


## Positions & Mechanics

### 1. Straddling the face (kneeling)
The receiver kneels over the giver's face, thighs bracketing the head, and lowers down. The most common and controllable.
- **Hovering** — receiver stays raised, giver reaches up with mouth and tongue. Less pressure, more teasing, receiver fully in control.
- **Settling** — receiver lowers their full weight (carefully, with hands braced). More smothering, more dominance.

### 2. Reverse / 69-top
The receiver faces the giver's feet (or faces away from the giver's gaze) — the "reverse" orientation. Changes the view and the access; can pair with a 69 if the giver is also being serviced.

### 3. Seated / throne (queening)
The receiver sits on a chair, couch edge, or bed edge with the giver kneeling or lying on the floor before them, head drawn in. The "queening" posture — the receiver enthroned, the giver kneeling at their feet. Pure worship/service framing.

### 4. Side / reclined
The giver lies back; the receiver sits astride their chest or stomach and shifts up. A gentler, more intimate angle; allows the receiver to lean forward and touch the giver at the same time.

## Power Dynamics

Facesitting's default is dominance from the receiver — but it can be tuned:

- **Worship / queening** — the giver is devoted, reverent; the receiver is adored, served, never degraded. "Use my mouth. Please."
- **Dominant / demanding** — the receiver takes what they want, gripping the giver's hair, grinding down, setting a ruthless pace.
- **Playful / teasing** — the receiver hovers just out of reach, lowers a little, pulls away; the giver chases. A game.
- **Tender / intimate** — slow, gentle, the receiver soft and undone, the giver patient and loving. Facesitting without the power edge.
- **Reversal / surprise** — a character who's usually submissive climbs on top and takes control, shocking both of them.


## Writing Tips

### Sensory checklist
- **Sight (receiver):** the giver's eyes looking up, hair between their fingers, the flush of the giver's face, the arch of their own back
- **Sight (giver):** mostly *not* sight — the close dark, a glimpse of the receiver's face far above, the curve of their stomach
- **Touch:** the press of thighs, the grip of hands, the weight, the heat, the grind
- **Sound:** muffled moans, the wet sounds of the mouth, the receiver's breath, the giver's breath going shallow
- **Scent/taste:** the receiver's arousal, skin, the heat between the thighs

### Vocabulary notes
- **Receiver:** *settle, lower, grind, roll, ride, press, hover, take, guide, grip, pull, moan, arch, tremble*
- **Giver:** *serve, worship, drink, lap, press, work, breathe, grip, surrender, hold, guide, lose (breath)*
- **The dynamic:** *above, beneath, weight, press, enclosure, surrender, devotion, control, use, please*

### Common pitfalls
1. **Forgetting the breathing.** The giver's air is restricted — write the shallow breaths, the pull-back for air, the "don't stop" through a gasp. It grounds the physical reality.
2. **Confusing the mechanics.** Keep the geometry clear: where are the receiver's knees? Is the giver's nose free? Does the receiver hover or sit? A sentence of setup prevents reader disorientation.
3. **Making it purely dominant.** Facesitting *can* be tender, playful, or worshipful. Match the tone to the characters, not a fixed stereotype.
4. **Ignoring safety.** Even in fiction, signal that the giver can breathe or tap out. A word or a squeeze on the thigh keeps the scene consensual and believable.
5. **No receiver agency.** The receiver is the active one here — don't write them as passive. Their hips, hands, and voice should *do* things.


## Scene Structure Example

1. **The Invitation** — "Come here." A pull of the hand, a smirk, or a soft, serious request. The giver is guided into place.

2. **The Mount** — The receiver straddles or lowers, thighs framing the giver's head. A beat of eye contact — or the deliberate closing of distance.

3. **The Settle** — Weight lowers. The first contact. The giver's hands find the receiver's thighs; the receiver's hand finds the giver's hair.

4. **The Ride** — Rhythm builds. The receiver grinds and rolls, finding their pace. The giver works, breathing hard between strokes. Moans from above feed into the mouth below.

5. **The Peak** — The receiver's control breaks: hips snapping, hand fisting hair, "don't stop, don't stop —" The giver holds on through it.

6. **The Release** — The receiver eases up, lets the giver breathe, collapses or pulls them up for a kiss. The aftermath: who thanks whom.
