# REMOTE-CONTROLLED & PUBLIC PLAY

## Overview

The remote-controlled vibrator is the ultimate **power-exchange toy**: one partner wears it, another controls it — from across the room, across the city, or across the world. The vibration is only the *wire*; the point is the **control, the secrecy, and the distance**. It turns arousal into something you can hand to someone else, and lets a partner touch you without touching you, in a room full of people who will never know.

The appeal lives in a specific, delicious paradox: the receiver is *falling apart* while *appearing perfectly composed*. Every pulse lands in secret. The controller holds the dial, and the receiver holds their face steady — until they can't.

**Key distinction:** This is not about the *sensation* of vibration (the main **sex-toys** skill covers buzz vs. rumble, toys, and materials). This is about the **scene** the toy creates: control at a distance, hidden arousal in public, and the war between composure and collapse. The vibrator is the prop; the power is the story.

- **The wearable** — an egg/insertable, a panty vibe, or a remote bullet, worn internally or nestled against the clitoris. It sits *hidden* under clothing; that hiddenness is half the tension.

## The Controller's Experience

- **The power of the dial** — deciding, withholding, escalating. The controller's thumb on the button is the whole dynamic.
- **The read** — watching the receiver's face and body for every tell, learning exactly which pulse makes them bite their lip. The controller is a *reader* first.

## The Receiver's Experience

- **The vulnerability** — their arousal is in someone else's hand, at someone else's whim. They didn't choose the timing; that's the surrender.

- **The unpredictability** — they don't know when the next pulse comes. The *not knowing* is the torture and the thrill.
- **The surrender** — the moment the pretense slips: the sharp inhale, the grip on the table, the "I can't — I can't keep doing this."

## Public Play Craft (the signature scene)

This is the scene the toy is *for*. Write it in layers:

- **The setting** — a dinner, a meeting, a movie, a party, a commute. The more *composed* the context, the sharper the contrast.
- **The timing** — the pulse lands at the worst possible moment: as they lift a glass, answer a question, sign their name, make eye contact with someone who mustn't know.
- **The tells** — the white knuckles, the bitten lip, the thigh pressed together, the sudden silence, the hand that covers a sound with a cough. The receiver's *fight* is the scene.
- **The near-slip** — a moan caught just in time, a glass set down too hard, a shiver that someone notices. "You okay? You look flushed." The recovery is the suspense.
- **The hidden/visible split** — everyone in the room sees a composed person; the reader sees the chaos underneath. Hold that split as long as you can — the longer the composure, the bigger the payoff.

## Writing Tips

### Sensory checklist
- **Touch:** the hidden buzz (low, near-silent, then rising), the wetness gathering under clothing, the tremor in the hands
- **Sight:** the receiver's flush, the white knuckles, the phone in the controller's hand, the toy slipped in and out of sight
- **Sound:** the faint hum only they can hear, the sharp inhale they can't fully swallow, the too-even voice
- **Interiority:** the receiver's war between composure and collapse; the controller's quiet thrill at the dial

### Vocabulary
- **The toy:** *remote, dial, pulse, pattern, buzz, hum, the app, the wearable, the egg, the wear, the test buzz*
- **The frame:** *hidden, secret, composure, the slip, the tell, control, permission, denial, distance, surrender, outlast, relentless*
- **The struggle:** *bite (the lip), white-knuckle, flush, tremble, catch (a moan), swallow (a sound), hold (the face steady), shiver*

### Common pitfalls
1. **Forgetting the secrecy** — public play without the "almost caught" tension is just a vibrator in a room. Write the near-slips.
2. **Receiver too composed** — the struggle is the scene. If they never crack, there's no tension. Show the tells.
3. **No controller interiority** — the controller's read and delight are half the heat. What does *holding the dial* feel like?
4. **Sensation over control** — the point is the power and the distance, not the vibration. Don't reduce it to "it felt good."
5. **Skipping the after** — the release of the pretense matters: the private collapse, the removal, the oversensitivity, the aftercare.

## Scene Structure Example

1. **The setup** — the toy goes in, the test buzz, the "act normal" instruction. A glance exchanged.
2. **The public stage** — the dinner, the meeting, the party. The receiver settled, composed, smiling.
3. **The first pulse** — low, hidden. The receiver's first flinch, caught and swallowed. The controller watches, smiling faintly.
4. **The escalation** — the dial rises, patterns shift, the pulses come at the worst moments. The tells multiply: the bitten lip, the knuckles, the too-careful answer.
5. **The almost caught** — a question ("You okay? You look flushed"), a glance that lingers, the desperate recovery.
6. **The release** — the controller grants it, or denies it until they're alone. The orgasm swallowed behind a hand, or the sprint to the door.
7. **The after** — the collapse, the removal, the pretense dropped. Who holds whom, now that it's over.
