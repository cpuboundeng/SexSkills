# SEX TOYS

## Overview

Sex toys are objects designed to produce or enhance sexual pleasure — and in smut, they're a toolbox for scenes that hands and mouths alone can't reach. A vibrator can produce a climax a character has never had before; a cock ring can make a scene hum with borrowed tension; an app-controlled toy can put control in a partner's hands from across a room. Toys add *capability*, *novelty*, and *vulnerability* to a scene.

The erotic appeal of toys in fiction is threefold. They **extend** what bodies can do (intensity, angles, hands-free stimulation). They **introduce power dynamics** (who holds the remote, who chose the toy, who's being "worked on"). And they **reveal character** — the nervous first-time toy user, the connoisseur with a drawerful, the partner thrilled or threatened by a machine that never tires.

**Key distinction:** Toys are *tools*, not replacements. The best toy scenes keep the characters central — the toy amplifies their desire, their control, their reaction. Write the *people*, not the plastic.

## Materials & Safety (the facts that make a scene feel real)

- **Body-safe materials:** platinum-cure silicone, glass, stainless steel, ABS plastic. Non-porous, easy to clean, won't harbor bacteria.
- **Avoid / caution:** jelly, TPE/TPR, PVC — porous, can't be fully sterilized, may leach plasticizers. A connoisseur character knows to avoid them; a cheap-toy scene can use them deliberately.
- **Lube compatibility:** silicone lube *degrades silicone toys* — use water-based lube with silicone toys. Oil-based lube degrades latex condoms. Glass and metal are lube-agnostic.
- **Cleaning:** soap and warm water for most; boiling or toy-cleaner for silicone; avoid submerging motorized bases.
- **Flared base for anal** — anything going in the rectum needs a flared base. This is the single most important safety fact, and it's non-negotiable in realistic scenes.
- **Condoms on toys** — sharing or easy cleanup; a responsible-partner detail.

## Toys in Scene Contexts

### Solo scenes
The toy as companion. The character's private ritual: charging it, cleaning it, the familiar weight, the first touch of vibration. A solo toy scene is a masturbation scene with a *characterful prop* — the toy says something about who they are (practical, indulgent, experimental, lonely).

### Partnered scenes
- **The giver wields the toy** — the toy becomes an extension of the giver's will, an implement of control. "Hold still." "Don't come until I say."
- **The receiver directs** — the receiver guides the toy, or hands it over, teaching their body to their partner.
- **The surprise** — the toy is revealed mid-scene ("I got us something"). The reaction — thrill or hesitation — is the story.

### Power dynamics
- **The remote** — one partner controls the other's pleasure from a distance. Public settings, restraint, or simply across the bed. Control is the point, not the sensation. See `references/remote_public_play.md` for the full scene guide — the hidden wearable, public composure play, and long-distance control.
- **The unyielding machine** — a toy never tires, never relents. It can "outlast" a human, which is both thrilling and, for some, intimidating — a jealousy/competition angle worth exploring.

### The "third partner" tension
Toys can read as a rival ("you like that more than me?") or as an ally ("let me use this on you"). Either reading is valid; the character's insecurity or generosity tells the reader everything.

## Writing Tips

### Sensory checklist
- **Sight:** the toy (sleek silicone, glinting glass, the wand's broad head), the receiver's body responding, the remote in a hand
- **Sound:** the *buzz* (high or low, loud or near-silent), the wetness it produces, the receiver's reactions
- **Touch:** vibration (buzzy vs. rumbly), the cool of glass, the soft firmness of silicone, the weight of a metal toy
- **Smell:** the faint new-silicone scent, clean toys, the musk of arousal

### Vocabulary
**Toys/actions:** *buzz, hum, pulse, vibrate, thrum, glide, press, insert, slide, cup, hold, angle, turn up, dial, remote, charge, warm, cool*
**Sensations:** *rumbly, buzzy, deep, intense, sharp, thrumming, relentless, pulsing, relentless, overwhelming, building, cresting*
**The power angle:** *control, dial, remote, command, permission, surrender, outlast, relentless, unyielding*

### Common pitfalls
1. **Forgetting the toy's physicality** — a toy has weight, texture, temperature, sound. Ground it. A "vibrator" with no described sensation is just a noun.
2. **The toy as magic orgasm button** — even strong toys don't produce instant, effortless climax for everyone. The build, the adjustment, the reaction still matter.
3. **Ignoring safety realism** — flared bases, lube compatibility, cleaning. Connoisseur characters know these; using them adds authenticity.
4. **Losing the people** — if the toy is doing all the work, the scene is a manual, not a story. Keep desire, control, and reaction human.
5. **No power dynamic** — the most interesting toy scenes hinge on *who's in control*. Decide that, and the scene writes itself.

## Scene Structure Example

1. **The Reveal** — The giver holds it up, or opens the box. The receiver's eyes widen. "Where did you get that?" A beat of anticipation.

2. **The Setup** — The toy is charged, cleaned, warmed. Lube, if needed. The ritual grounds the scene in reality.

3. **The First Touch** — The first buzz against skin. The receiver's reaction — a gasp, a laugh, a full-body flinch. The giver watches, learning their body's answer.

4. **The Dial** — The giver turns it up, or hands over the remote, or presses it firmer. The receiver's breathing changes. Control asserted or surrendered.

5. **The Build** — The unrelenting thrum does what a hand can't sustain. The receiver climbs, desperate, surprised by the speed. The giver doesn't let up.

6. **The Peak** — The receiver breaks under the vibration — the toy pushed against them, held firm through the climax, drawing it out past the point they thought they could take.

7. **The Aftermath** — The toy clicks off. The sudden silence. The receiver limp, oversensitive, laughing weakly. The giver sets it aside and reaches for *them* instead.
