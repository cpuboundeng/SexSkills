# THREESOME

## Overview

A threesome is any sexual encounter involving three people simultaneously. The core challenge is **geometry, not complexity**: a third body doubles the number of active contact points, so the key is assigning each person a clear, active role so no one drifts into passivity. Unlike pair sex with one axis of movement, a threesome has multiple overlapping axes — the choreography must keep everyone engaged across all of them.

The erotic appeal lies in intensification: more hands, more mouths, more angles, more bodies watching and reacting. The visual dimension adds voyeurism and exhibitionism. Power dynamics shift — two against one, alternating attention, shared or divided desire — creating tension that solo or dyadic play cannot replicate. Surveys show **52% of straight women, 65% of queer women, and 66% of straight/queer men** have fantasized about threesomes; approximately **30% of adults** in North America have experienced one.

**Key distinction:** A threesome is not two separate pairs having sex side by side. It is a single scene where all three bodies are actively connected — even if the connections alternate in focus.

## Common Pitfalls

### Choreography Mistakes
1. **The orphaned partner:** One person ends up passively watching. Fix: assign them an active task — touch, hold a toy, direct, or take the center position.
2. **Dead air during rotation:** Long pauses when partners switch positions kill momentum. Fix: overlap — the incoming partner starts before the outgoing one finishes.
3. **Center partner burnout:** The partner at the center may get overstimulated or fatigued. Fix: build in rotation; let the center partner become a giver for a period.
4. **Ignoring the secondary axis:** Only one pair interacts while the third watches. Fix: create moments where the third and one other partner connect — a kiss, a touch, shared eye contact.

### Communication Mistakes
5. **Assuming preferences translate from pair sex:** What works for two doesn't always work for three. Check in.
6. **Forgetting the third person's needs:** Especially with a "unicorn" (the single third joining a couple), their desires may be secondary. Actively address this.
7. **Jealousy unaddressed:** One partner may feel left out or competitive. Normal — but call it out gently if you notice it.

### Physical Mistakes
8. **Space crunch:** Three bodies in a queen bed can feel tight. Fix: use the floor, a larger bed, or positions that stack rather than spread.
9. **Lube shortage:** More bodies = more friction. Keep a fresh bottle within reach; don't wait until it's dry.
10. **Height mismatch ignored:** Don't force a position that doesn't work ergonomically. A pillow under the central partner is the single most effective fix.
11. **Condom cross-contamination:** In MMF/MFM, males switching partners need a fresh condom. Have extras ready.

## Safety & Comfort Notes

- **Safe word:** Agree on one before starting. Use it for physical pain, emotional overwhelm, or boundary crossing.
- **Alcohol:** Keep it moderate. Intoxication impairs communication and body awareness — critical in a three-body geometry.
- **Toys:** Clean between partners. Share with gloves or condoms if STI status is mixed.

- **Aftercare for all three:** The receiving partner(s) often need aftercare. So do the givers — three people giving intense attention can be physically and emotionally draining. Check on everyone.
