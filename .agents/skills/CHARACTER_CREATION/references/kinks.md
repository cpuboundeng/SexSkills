# KINKS

## Overview

A kink is more than a box on a checklist — it's a *window*. What a person is into reveals what they want to *feel*: powerful or helpless, cherished or degraded, seen or anonymous, in control or finally free of it. The craft is to build a kink profile around that feeling, so a character's kinks read as *psychology*, not as a menu.

**Key distinction:** A kink is the act; the *feeling it produces* is the point. "Likes being tied up" is a fact. "Being tied up is the only time she can stop running the world and just *feel*" is a character. Always connect the kink to the feeling — and the feeling to the person.

## The Kink Profile

For each kink a character has, answer four things:

1. **What** — the kink itself (the act, the dynamic, the fantasy).
2. **Why** — the emotional need it meets, and the backstory hook that explains it.
3. **How they feel about it** — proud, ashamed, curious, matter-of-fact. Their *attitude* toward their own desire is half the character.
4. **Limits** — their hard and soft limits. A character's boundaries are characterization, not a buzzkill.

## Kink as Need (map the feeling)

Every common kink tends to point at an emotional need. This mapping is the single most useful tool for making kinks feel *deep* rather than *decorative*:

| Kink | The feeling it usually delivers |
|------|--------------------------------|
| **Submission / being dominated** | relief from control, being cared for, the freedom of not deciding |
| **Dominance** | being trusted with power, competence, being *chosen* to lead |
| **Exhibitionism / being watched** | being seen, validated, made the center of attention |
| **Voyeurism / watching** | safety at a distance, the thrill of the forbidden without the risk |
| **Degradation / humiliation** | the relief of being "nothing" for a while, of surrendering pride |
| **Praise** | being told you're good, enough, wanted — the antidote to a lifetime of "not enough" |
| **Breeding / claiming** | being wanted *permanently*, being kept, belonging |
| **Service** | the peace of a clear purpose, of being useful, of devotion |
| **Impact / pain** | sensation that crowds out thought, catharsis, the proof of intensity |
| **CNC / ravishment** | wanting it so much that the "no" is a fantasy of not having to choose (see **bdsm**) |
| **Age / caregiver dynamics** | being safe, being looked after, the relief of being small |

The move: **write the need, not just the act.** "She likes being tied up *because it's the only time she isn't responsible for everything*" — now the kink is a story.

## The Why (the backstory hook)

Give the kink an origin — a specific, small explanation that makes it feel inevitable:

- *"She spent her whole life in charge, so surrendering is the only time she exhales."*
- *"Nobody ever called him good at anything, so praise undoes him."*
- *"He grew up invisible, so being watched is the first time he's felt real."*

The hook can be a single line. It doesn't need to be tragic or elaborate — it just needs to *connect* the kink to the person. (Mystery is fine too: a character who doesn't know *why* they need this can be just as compelling.)

## Kink as Character Revelation

The best kink scenes aren't about the act — they're about the *moment a character admits or acts on their kink*. That's where the person is revealed:

- **The discovery** — the first time they realize they like this (with horror, or relief).
- **The confession** — saying it out loud, terrified, and being *met* with acceptance (or not).
- **The shame/relief hinge** — the fear that "this makes me wrong" tipping into "this makes me *me*."

Write the reveal. It's the emotional core of kink fiction — the kink is just the vehicle.

## Pairing Kinks for Chemistry

Most kinks are *interpersonal* — they need a counterpart. This is where two characters become a scene:

- **Complementary** — dom/sub, exhibitionist/voyeur, caregiver/little, brat/tamer. The kinks fit; the heat is smooth and mutual.
- **Conflicting** — two doms, a vanilla partner with a kinky one, a character whose kink the other has to *learn*. The friction is the story.
- **The unexpected** — the stoic who's secretly submissive, the brat who's secretly a caretaker, the big scary one who needs praise. A kink that *contradicts* the surface is the most interesting kind.
- **The mirror** — two characters with the *same* wound and *opposite* kinks (one needs control, the other needs to give it up). They complete each other.

## Limits & Consent (framing)

- **Hard limits** — things a character will never do. **Soft limits** — things they're nervous about, might try with trust. Both are *characterization*: what a person won't do says as much as what they will.
- **Consent as heat.** Negotiation, the ask, the check-in — these aren't buzzkills, they're *texture* that raises the temperature. A kink given freely is hotter than a kink taken. (See the `skill-style-guide` and `bdsm`.)

- **Every act skill** (blowjob, anal, group-sex, watersports, etc.) — the *content* of the kink.
- **verbal-erotica** — dirty talk is how kinks get *spoken*.

## Vocabulary

- **The frame:** *into, kink, fetish, turn-on, needs, craves, can't get off without, would do anything for, secretly wants*
- **The feeling:** *power, surrender, relief, freedom, being seen, being held, being nothing, being everything, being kept, being good*
- **The reveal:** *admit, confess, discover, realize, blurt, whisper, be met with, be accepted*

## Common Pitfalls

1. **Kink as checklist** — a list of acts with no feeling or why behind them. Connect to the need.
2. **No origin** — a kink that appears from nowhere. Give it a hook (or a deliberate mystery).
3. **Kinks that never show up** — stated in the profile, absent from the scene. Show them.
4. **Reducing the character to one kink** — "the sub," "the exhibitionist." A kink is a facet, not a personality.
5. **Forgetting limits** — a character with no boundaries has no edge and no realism. Limits are characterization.
6. **Skipping the reveal** — jumping to the act without the moment of admission. The reveal is the heart.
