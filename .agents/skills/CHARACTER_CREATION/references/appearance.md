# APPEARANCE

## Overview

Appearance is the first thing a reader "sees" of a character — and the first place smut writing goes wrong, by turning it into a head-to-toe catalog or a pile of "beautiful/handsome" with no content. The craft is to make a character *visible and specific*, and to describe them through the eyes of someone who wants them.

**Key distinction:** Don't describe appearance as a static portrait. Describe it as a person *being looked at* — noticed, wanted, remembered. Appearance lives in the POV character's attention, not in a police report.

## Hair

- **Color** — black, brown, chestnut, auburn, red, strawberry, honey, blond, ash, gray, silver, white — or dyed (blue, pink, the fading roots of a past dye job). A hair color can be a character's *signature*.
- **Texture** — straight, wavy, curly, coily, fine, thick, coarse. (See `race_ethnicity.md` for respectful texture language.)
- **Length & style** — cropped, shoulder-length, long, a braid, locs, a bun, loose, pinned up, always falling in their face. How they *wear* it says something about them.
- **How to write it:** hair is always *moving* — catching light, sticking to a sweaty neck, pulled loose, pushed back, fisted in a hand. Write it in motion, not as a swatch.

## Eyes

- **Color** — blue, green, hazel, gray, amber, brown, nearly black. Color matters less than *the look*.
- **The look** — this is where the character lives. Eyes that crinkle when they laugh, that go dark with want, that watch too carefully, that can't meet someone's. A gaze is characterization.
- **How to write it:** the eye contact (held, broken, avoided), the glance, the slow look up and down. Eyes are the doorway to interiority — what they *look at* reveals what they want.

## Skin

- **Tone** — see `race_ethnicity.md` for respectful, specific tone vocabulary.
- **Texture & warmth** — smooth, rough, warm, cool, sun-warmed, goosebumped. How skin *reacts* is the erotic part.
- **Reactions** — the flush, the blush creeping down the neck, the sweat, the goosebumps, the heat rising. A body that *shows* its arousal is the most alive description there is.

## Face & Features

- **The shape of the face** — the jaw, the cheekbones, the brow. Sharp or soft, angular or round.
- **The mouth** — the single most-watched feature in smut. The curve of the lips, the way they part, the smile (real, crooked, rare), the bitten lip, the mouth going slack. A mouth is a character.
- **The nose, the neck, the hands** — the details a lover actually notices: the hollow of the throat, the line of the jaw, the hands (always the hands — how they move, what they do).

## Presence (the part that isn't visible)

- **How they move** — deliberately or loosely, with grace or with a shambling ease.
- **How they sound** — the register of the voice, the laugh, the breath. Voice is appearance, too.
- **How they smell** — soap, smoke, perfume, clean sweat, the particular scent of *them*. Scent is the sense most tied to memory and desire.
- **How they occupy a room** — do they fill it, shrink from it, command it without trying?

## Show, Don't Tell (the core technique)

Replace the label with the *evidence* — and let the POV character's reaction be the description:

| Telling | Showing |
|---------|---------|
| "She was beautiful" | "The light caught the sweat at her collarbone, and he forgot what he was going to say." |
| "He was handsome" | "She noticed the way his shirt pulled at the shoulders, and looked away before he caught her." |
| "She had nice eyes" | "Her eyes were the color of good whiskey, and they didn't leave his." |

The trick: **the POV character noticing IS the description.** You don't need to narrate beauty — you need to show someone being unable to look away.

## Vocabulary

- **Hair:** *dark, fair, silver-shot, curling, straight, soft, thick, loose, pinned, mussed, falling, sticking*
- **Eyes:** *dark, bright, warm, heavy-lidded, sharp, soft, watchful, crinkled, hooded, dilated, blown (with want)*
- **Skin:** *warm, flushed, smooth, sun-browned, freckled, scarred, glowing, slick (with sweat), goosebumped*
- **Face/mouth:** *the jaw, the throat, the hollow, the curve of the lip, the bitten lip, the rare smile, the mouth going soft/slack*
- **Presence:** *deliberate, loose, graceful, heavy, quiet, magnetic, easy, restless, still*

## Common Pitfalls

1. **The catalog** — hair, then eyes, then height, then build, in a list. Drip details through the scene instead.
2. **"Beautiful/handsome" with nothing behind it** — a label isn't a description. Show the specific thing.
3. **Everyone described the same way** — if every love interest is "tall, dark, and handsome," none of them is a person. Vary it.
4. **Forgetting the POV filter** — describing objectively instead of through someone's hungry attention.
5. **Static portrait** — a character standing still being described. Show them *moving, reacting, looking*.
