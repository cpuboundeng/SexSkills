# PERSONALITY & BACKSTORY

## Overview

Personality and backstory are *why* a character is the way they are — and what their desire is made of. A body and a kink list are just surfaces until they're attached to a person with a history, a wound, and a voice. This is where a character goes from "hot" to "someone I'm rooting for."

**Key distinction:** Backstory is *fuel*, not *exposition*. You don't need a biography chapter. You need a few specific past moments that explain the present — and you reveal them in glimpses, at the moment they matter.

## Personality

- **Traits** — warm, guarded, playful, intense, dry, reckless, careful, vain, generous, envious, loyal. Pick two or three strong ones, not a full Myers-Briggs.
- **Flaws (the good kind)** — pride, fear, jealousy, people-pleasing, avoidance, a temper, a habit of self-sabotage. Flaws are what make a character *specific* and what create obstacles. A flawless character has nothing to overcome and nothing to learn.
- **The contradiction** — the trait that shouldn't fit, and that makes them feel human: the dom who's tender after, the confident flirt who freezes at real intimacy, the stoic who writes love letters. One contradiction is worth ten consistent traits.

## The Desire / Secret / Wound Triad

This is the heart of a character. Three things, braided together:

1. **Desire** — what they *want* (from the main skill's desire engine).
2. **Secret** — what they *won't admit* wanting, or feeling, or being. The shame that makes the desire hot.
3. **Wound** — the past hurt that *explains* the desire and the secret. The wound is the origin story.

**The braid:** the wound created the secret, and the secret shapes the desire.

- *Wound:* his ex said he was "too much" and left. *Secret:* he's terrified of wanting too openly. *Desire:* he craves someone who asks him to stay. Now his eagerness — the thing he was shamed for — becomes the thing his lover treasures. That's a character, not a list.

Write the wound *lightly* — one or two concrete past moments, not a trauma résumé. The reader should *feel* the wound through the present behavior, not be told about it.

## Backstory as Fuel (not info-dump)

- **A few specific moments, not a life story.** "She learned to cook from her grandmother" tells us more than a paragraph of genealogy — *if* it shows up as a detail (she cooks when she's nervous).
- **Reveal it at the point of relevance.** The backstory matters when it *explains the present* — why they flinch at a raised voice, why they can't accept a compliment, why they never stay the night.
- **Let the reader infer.** Don't say "he had a bad childhood." Show him checking that the door is locked three times, or going quiet when someone yells. The behavior *is* the backstory.

## Voice

A character's voice — in dialogue and inner monologue — should sound like *them*, and no one else:

- **Word choice** — the vocabulary they reach for. A professor's "precisely" vs. a mechanic's "yep." Class, region, and personality all live in word choice.
- **Rhythm** — short and clipped, or long and rambling; jokes, or none; silence, or a wall of words.
- **What they never say** — the things they dodge, the names they won't use, the subject they change. Avoidance is voice, too.
- **The inner voice** — how they narrate their own life (self-critical, amused, panicked, tender). Interiority is voice. (See `skill-style-guide` and `verbal-erotica`.)

## Archetypes (a spark, not a cage)

Start from a recognizable shape, then *individualize it into a person*:

- **The caretaker** — lives to tend others; needs someone to tend *them*. Desire: to be needed.
- **The brat** — provokes to get a reaction; secretly wants to be caught and held. Desire: to be worth chasing.
- **The stoic** — feelings locked down; one crack in the armor is the whole story. Desire: to be safe enough to feel.
- **The golden retriever** — open, eager, loyal; the wound is being taken for granted. Desire: to be wanted back just as hard.
- **The wounded** — guarded and slow to trust; the wall comes down by degrees. Desire: to be proven wrong about people.
- **The confident flirt** — performs ease; terrified of the real thing. Desire: someone who sees through the performance.
- **The reluctant** — says no, means yes-not-yet; the give is the scene. Desire: permission to want.

Use these as *starting points*, then give the character specifics that don't fit the label. The brat who's secretly a caretaker; the stoic who's funny when no one's watching. That's where people live.

## Want vs. Need (the deeper engine)

- **Want** is what they *chase* — a person, a feeling, a fix.
- **Need** is what would actually *heal* them — often the thing they're running from.
- The friction between want and need is where a character becomes a *story*. They chase the wrong thing until the scene forces them to see what they actually need.

## Common Pitfalls

1. **Flat archetype** — a brat who's only a brat. Add the contradiction, the secret, the specificity.
2. **Info-dump backstory** — a wall of past events that never connects to the present.
3. **No wound** — a character with no past hurt has no reason for their walls, and no arc to walk.
4. **Perfect character** — no flaws, nothing to overcome. Boring and unbelievable.
5. **Same voice for everyone** — two characters who sound identical. Voice is characterization; vary it.
6. **Backstory that never pays off** — a wound that's mentioned and never touched again. Use it or cut it.
