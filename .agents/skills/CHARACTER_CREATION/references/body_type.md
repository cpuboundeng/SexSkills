# BODY TYPE

## Overview

Bodies are wildly diverse — and that diversity is the *material*, not a problem to smooth over. The single "conventionally perfect" default is boring and excludes most readers. The craft is to write the **specific body** — this one, with its particular shape, size, softness, and marks — and to write it as *desired*, so the reader sees why this exact body is irresistible to the POV character.

**Key distinction:** This is not a catalog of measurements. It's about writing a body the way a lover would experience it — through touch, weight, warmth, and want — not the way a doctor or a tailor would.

## Body Shapes & Their Appeal

Every shape has its own feel, and its own *appeal* to the person who wants it. Describe the appeal, not just the silhouette:

| Shape | The feel | The appeal to write |
|-------|----------|---------------------|
| **Slim / slender / petite** | light, delicate, angular | the curve of a collarbone, the waist a hand can nearly span, the grace of it |
| **Athletic / toned** | firm, defined, capable | the shift of muscle under skin, the strength, the way they *move* |
| **Curvy / hourglass** | full, soft, dramatic | the dip of the waist, the hips that fill a grip, the lushness |
| **Plus-size / full-figured / soft** | abundant, warm, giving | softness that yields and holds, the weight, the warmth, the generosity of the body |
| **Muscular / strong** | powerful, solid, dense | the bulk of shoulders, the definition, the sense of being *handled* or *held* by it |
| **Stocky / built** | sturdy, grounded, substantial | solidity, the heft, the unshakeable presence |
| **Tall / lanky** | long, sprawling, rangy | the length of the legs, the reach, the way they fold themselves down to you |
| **Short / compact** | contained, concentrated | the intensity, the way they fit against someone, the low center of gravity |

The trick: **name the shape, then write the sensation and the want.** "Curvy" is a label; "her hips filled his hands, and he wanted to stay there" is a body.

## Breasts (size, shape, color — written as desired)

Breasts are a primary focus in much smut, so write them with specificity and warmth — never as a clinical measurement.

- **Size** — small, modest, full, generous, large. Whatever the size, write it as *attractive to the POV character*: a small chest can be delicate and sensitive; a full chest can be lush and heavy. No size is a flaw.
- **Shape** — teardrop, round, full on top or bottom, perky, soft/hanging, wide-set, close-set, asymmetrical. **Asymmetry is normal and can be written as endearing or beautiful**, not as a defect to correct.
- **Color** — nipples and areolas vary enormously: pink, rose, tan, brown, deep brown — often darker than the surrounding skin, and darkening further with arousal. Describe the *contrast* ("darker nipples against pale skin," "a warm brown against the deep tone of her chest") as part of a person's body, the way a lover would notice.
- **Movement & response** — the most alive detail: how they move with breath, how the nipple hardens and stands, how they flatten when she lies back, how the skin flushes. Write the *response*, not the spec.

**The rule:** cup size is the least interesting thing about a breast. Write how it feels, weighs, moves, and responds — and how the POV character can't stop looking.

## The Marks That Make a Body Specific

What makes a body feel *real* (and *loved*) is rarely its proportions — it's the specific details:

- **Body hair** — chest hair, happy trail, legs, arms, a soft stomach. Write it as texture and warmth, not as something to be removed.
- **Scars** — each one is a story. A character whose partner traces an old scar is a character being *known*.
- **Stretch marks** — on hips, thighs, stomach, breasts. Write them as part of a loved body, never as a flaw.
- **Freckles, moles, birthmarks** — the constellation on a shoulder, the mole by the mouth that the POV character keeps kissing.
- **Tattoos & piercings** — what they chose to put on their body, and why. A tattoo is a clue to the person.

These are the details that make a body *this* body, and they're where tenderness lives.

## Writing the Body as Loved, Not Judged

- **Filter through the POV character's desire.** The reader doesn't need an objective list; they need to see the body through someone who *wants* it. "The soft curve of her stomach" is desire; "she carried a little extra weight" is judgment.
- **Even "flaws" are turn-ons.** In the right eyes, a soft belly is something to grip; a scar is a story to trace; a big nose is a profile to memorize. The POV character's hunger *reframes* the body.
- **Show the body in motion and response.** Bodies are hottest when they're *doing something* — breathing, arching, sweating, shaking. Describe the living body, not a still portrait.

## Vocabulary

- **Shapes:** *slim, slender, petite, lithe, athletic, toned, lean, curvy, hourglass, full, soft, plush, generous, plus-size, muscular, strong, built, stocky, solid, tall, lanky, rangy, short, compact, small*
- **Breasts:** *small, modest, full, generous, heavy, soft, pert, teardrop, round, the swell, the curve, the weight of them; the nipple, the areola, hardening, pebbling, standing*
- **Marks:** *scar, stretch mark, freckle, mole, birthmark, tattoo, piercing, body hair, the trail of hair, the softness, the plane, the dip, the hollow*
- **How a body is experienced:** *soft, firm, warm, solid, yielding, giving, strong, heavy, light, the weight, the heft, the shape of them under a hand*

## Common Pitfalls

1. **One body type only** — every character is tall, toned, and flawless. Diversify shapes; each has its own appeal.
2. **Clinical measurement** — cup sizes, exact weights, BMI. A lover doesn't think in specs.
3. **Fat-shaming or flaw-framing** — writing softness, scars, or stretch marks as something to apologize for. Write them as loved.
4. **Judging instead of desiring** — the narrator describing the body critically instead of through the POV character's want.
5. **Generic "perfect"** — a flawless body with no specific marks is forgettable. Give it a scar, a freckle, a specific shape.
