
# SEXTING

## Overview

Sexting is erotic communication at a distance — words, images, and voice exchanged through phones and screens to build arousal without touch. It encompasses the dirty text thread, the voice memo, the phone call, the photo, the video clip — any erotic exchange where the body is absent and the imagination does the work.

Its appeal is the *gap*. There is no touch, no sight of the whole, no shared room — only words and the reader's (and characters') imagination to fill the space. That gap is the engine: what's *not* said, what's *hinted*, what the recipient pictures between the lines, is often hotter than any explicit description. Sexting is the purest form of erotic *anticipation* — the arousal lives in the waiting, the typing bubble, the "read" receipt, the distance.

**Key distinction:** Sexting is not dirty talk typed out. Dirty talk happens *in* the moment, to a present partner. Sexting happens *across* a distance, to an imagined one — and its craft is about pacing, implication, and the rhythm of the exchange, not just the words themselves. (See `references/dirty_talk.md` for the verbal content, which this skill builds on and extends to a new medium.)


## The Psychology

- **Anticipation** — the wait for the reply, the typing bubble, the "read" with no response. Time itself becomes arousal.
- **The imagination gap** — absence forces invention. The recipient pictures everything the words imply; less is more.
- **Safety & distance** — the screen is a shield; people say things they'd struggle to say face-to-face. Sexting lowers inhibition.
- **Control of the self** — you choose the words, the timing, the photo, the angle. You curate your own desire and send it.
- **The secret** — sexts live in the phone; the thrill of the hidden, the deletable, the "what if someone saw."


## The Mediums

### 1. Text
The dominant form. Words only (plus emoji, timing, and the metadata of typing bubbles, read receipts, and gaps).
- **Strengths:** precision, pacing, revisiting, the slow burn across hours.
- **Craft:** short messages, deliberate gaps, escalation over time, the power of the unanswered question.

### 2. Images / photos
A picture of a body, a tease, a suggestion. Often partial — a collarbone, a shadow, a hand on a thigh, a "you wish you were here" angle.
- **The tease photo** — shows little, implies much. The half-buttoned shirt, the sheet pulled low, the fogged mirror.
- **The escalation** — photos grow more revealing as the conversation heats, or stay deliberately withheld.

### 3. Voice notes / audio
The voice without the face. Breathing, the catch in a word, the smile audible in a sentence.
- **Strengths:** intimacy (the voice is the most personal thing you can send), tone, sound.
- **Craft:** the pause, the lowered voice, the "I'm touching myself" whispered with a breath behind it.

### 4. Phone call / live audio
Sexting without the screen — real-time voice, no visual, the listener supplying everything.
- **Strengths:** immediacy, the feedback of live breath and moans, the absence of the "edit."
- **Craft:** describe less, sound more. The listener's imagination is the stage. (See `references/dirty_talk.md` for phone scenes.)

### 5. Video / live
The closest to presence — real-time sight and sound across distance.
- **Strengths:** the visual, the performance, the "show me" of it.
- **Craft:** the angle, the performance for the camera, the mutual watching.


## The Craft: Pacing the Exchange

Sexting is a *rhythm*, not a monologue. The craft is in the back-and-forth:

### 1. The opening (the first spark)
- A line that opens a door: "I can't stop thinking about you." "What are you wearing?" (a cliché — use with intent) or better, "I saw something today that made me think of you."
- The opening should *invite*, not demand. It leaves room for the other to step in.

### 2. The escalation (turn by turn)
- Each message raises the stakes slightly: flirty → suggestive → explicit → descriptive → the photo/the voice note.
- Escalate *gradually*. Jumping to explicit too fast skips the tension.
- Match your partner's pace — escalation is a duet, not a sprint.

### 3. The gap (the most powerful tool)
- The pause before replying. The typing bubble that appears and stops. The "read" left hanging.
- The gap *is* the anticipation. A well-timed delay says more than words.
- Write the gaps — the character staring at the phone, thumb hovering, heart racing.

### 4. The turn (power in the exchange)
- Who leads? Sexting is a dance of control: one leads, the other responds, then flips.
- The teasing withdrawal — going quiet, making the other beg for more.
- The command — "send me a picture" / "tell me what you'd do."

### 5. The peak (the payoff)
- The exchange reaches its hottest point — the explicit description, the photo, the live voice — and then resolves.
- The payoff can be mutual (both finishing, on the phone or after) or *deferred* (the promise: "when I get home…").

### 6. The landing (after the heat)
- The comedown text: the soft "I miss you," the laugh, the "that was… wow."
- The intimacy that persists after the arousal — the "goodnight" that means more.


## Writing Tips

### Write the *phone* as part of the scene
The device is a character. The typing bubble, the read receipt, the screenshot, the deleting-and-rewriting, the phone clutched to the chest, the screen's glow in a dark room. These details ground the scene and build tension.

### Show both sides (if you have both)
Cut between the sender and the receiver — the sender's hesitation ("is this too much?"), the receiver's reaction ("oh god, oh god"). The two-sidedness of a sext exchange is its structure.

### Less is more
The imagination gap is the point. A half-sentence ("I would —" ) left unfinished is hotter than a full explicit paragraph. Trust the reader (and the characters) to fill it in.

### Use timing as prose
The gap, the delay, the instant reply — all of these are *plot beats* in a sexting scene. A message that takes three minutes to arrive (and the reason why) is a scene.

### Sensory checklist
- **Sight:** the phone screen, the glow in the dark, the photo (partial, suggestive), the reflection in a window
- **Sound:** the notification chime, the typing, the voice note's breath, the low voice on a call
- **Touch:** the phone in the hand, the thumb hovering, the hand wandering to one's own body
- **Interiority:** the anticipation, the second-guessing, the arousal building alone in a room, the imagination racing

### Vocabulary notes
- **The send:** *type, delete, retype, send, wait, hold breath, screenshot, save*
- **The wait:** *typing bubble, read receipt, no reply, three dots, the gap, the pause*
- **The tease:** *imply, hint, suggest, "you'd have to see," "wish you were here," half, almost*
- **The escalation:** *tell me, show me, what would you do, I want, I would, when I get home*

### Common pitfalls
1. **Writing it as one long monologue.** Sexting is an *exchange*. Two voices, turn by turn, with gaps.
2. **Ignoring the medium.** A sexting scene that never mentions the phone, the wait, the screen is just dialogue with extra steps.
3. **Too explicit too fast.** Skip the escalation and you skip the point. The build is the kink.
4. **No gaps.** Continuous back-and-forth with no pauses has no tension. The delay is the drumbeat.
5. **Forgetting the loneliness.** Sexting is *alone together* — each character is in their own room, their own body. That solitary heat is part of the appeal.


## Scene Structure Example

1. **The Spark** — A mundane context (separate cities, late night, a boring meeting). The first message arrives, opening a door.

2. **The Dance** — Turn and turn, escalating. A joke becomes a hint becomes an invitation. The typing bubble appears and stops.

3. **The Gap** — One goes quiet. The other stares at the screen, heart racing, thumb hovering. The "read" hangs.

4. **The Turn** — A photo. A voice note. The low "tell me." The exchange tips from playful to hungry.

5. **The Peak** — The heat peaks across the distance — live, or in words — and resolves.

6. **The Landing** — The comedown, the soft message, the promise for later, the phone clutched in the dark.
