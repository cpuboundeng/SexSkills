## The Craft of Writing It

### Dialogue rules for smut
1. **Keep it short.** Real dirty talk is terse. Long speeches read as monologue, not arousal. A breath can carry more than a sentence.
2. **Interrupt it.** Body language cuts dialogue: a gasp swallowing a word, a kiss ending a sentence, a thrust breaking a voice.
3. **Repeat and echo.** Callbacks ("say it again," "louder") build ritual and rhythm.
4. **Use names and titles.** A name, "sir," "baby," "good girl" — specificity grounds the line in *these two people*.
5. **Let silence speak.** The pause before an answer is as charged as the answer.

### Matching words to character
- **What would *this* person actually say?** A buttoned-up character's dirty talk will be halting, shocked at their own words. A confident Dom's will be smooth and assured. Voice is characterization.
- **Register shift is the tell.** The moment a polite character breaks into filth is the scene's turn. Make that shift deliberate.

### Tone calibration
| Tone | Words | Rhythm |
|------|-------|--------|
| **Tender** | want, need, love, good, mine, please | soft, slow, murmured |
| **Dominant** | say it, beg, take, obey, mine, now | short, clipped, unyielding |
| **Submissive** | please, yes, yours, more, don't stop | breathless, pleading, broken |
| **Playful** | bet, tease, what if, make me | laughing, bantering, light |
| **Desperate** | please, now, can't, need, fuck | urgent, gasping, fragmented |

### Punctuation as breath
Use dashes and ellipses to show voice breaking:
- "I — I can't — please —"
- "Come for me. Now."
- "Don't you *dare* stop."

## Writing Tips

### Sensory checklist
- **Sound:** the timbre of the voice (low, breathy, strained, cracked), volume shifts, the smile audible in a word, the gasp between syllables
- **Sight:** lips moving, eye contact while speaking, the mouth forming a word
- **Touch:** a whispered word against skin, breath against the ear, a hand tightening as a word lands
- **Interiority:** what the character *doesn't* say — the line caught in their throat

### Common pitfalls
1. **Over-writing** — too many words. Erotic dialogue should be spare.
2. **Generic voice** — if any character could say the line, it's not *this* character. Specificity is everything.
3. **No reaction beats** — dirty talk lands hardest when the *other* person reacts. Show the effect of the words.
4. **Purple clichés** — avoid worn phrases unless the character would genuinely use them. Earn the filthy line.
5. **Forgetting consent's frame** — degradation and commands read as abuse without an agreed context. The *structure* (a safeword, a nod, a "yes") is part of the eroticism.
