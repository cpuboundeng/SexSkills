# Age Play Guide

This reference covers little/caregiver dynamics, elements, and safety for the BDSM skill.

## What It Is

Age play involves one or both partners adopting a younger persona — a headspace of care, innocence, and simplicity, with a caregiver (often but not always the Dominant) providing nurturing authority. It is a consensual, chosen roleplay between adults. The appeal is usually not "acting young" so much as the *headspace*: the relief of not being responsible, the safety of being cared for, the simplicity of a world where someone else decides. For many littles it's comfort and healing; for others it's pure play.

## Age Play Types

| Type | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **Little (younger sub)** | Childlike persona — playful, innocent, care-receiving |
| **Caregiver (older Dom)** | Nurturing, authoritative caretaker |
| **Middle** | Tween persona — bratty, independent, in-between |
| **Teen** | Teenager persona — rebellious, dramatic |
| **Young/young (YY)** | Both partners play younger — peer play |
| **Petite** | Treated as smaller than they are; size difference play |

## Little Space vs. Headspace

- **Little space**: the state the sub enters — playful, soft, carefree; distinct from *acting* young
- **Caregiver headspace**: the nurturing, protective mental state of the Dom — its own altered state
- **Not regression**: most age players are not psychologically regressing — it's a chosen headspace with an on/off switch (the switch matters; see safety)
- **The spectrum**: some scenes are all comfort (bottles, bedtime, snuggles); some blend discipline and care (time-outs, spankings); some are non-sexual entirely

## Age Play Elements

| Element | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Bottles/sippy cups** | Drinking as a younger persona — milk, juice, or other drinks |
| **Pacifiers** | Oral comfort; reduces speech |
| **Bibs** | Feeding ritual; signals the role |
| **Toys** | Stuffed animals, rattles, coloring, blocks |
| **Crawling** | Physical movement at a younger age |
| **Baby talk / soft speech** | Simplified or playful speech — never mocking |
| **Discipline** | Time-outs, corner time, spanking, grounding |
| **Rewards** | Stickers, treats, praise charts |
| **Bedtime routine** | Bath, story, pajamas, tucking in |
| **Dressing** | Onesies, footed pajamas, age-appropriate clothing |

## Scene Ideas

- The caregiver feeds the little a bottle while the little wears a bib and clutches a stuffed animal
- Time-out for misbehavior: the little sits in the corner while the caregiver watches
- Sticker chart: stickers earned for good behavior, traded for a treat
- Bath time: the caregiver bathes, dries, and dresses the little
- Bedtime: story, tucking in, a kiss on the forehead, the light left on
- Discipline scene: the caregiver's firmness after a boundary is tested

## Safety & Considerations

- **Consent to discipline**: some littles love spankings/time-outs; others want pure comfort — clarify which, and never blur the two
- **Headspace is a switch**: entering little space should be gradual and consensual; *exiting* needs gentle support — abrupt exits feel jarring and wrong
- **The sexual element is separate**: not all age play is sexual, and the sexual parts are negotiated like any other kink — the innocent elements are not an excuse for sexual content outside consent
- **Privacy**: age play is stigmatized; participants decide who knows, always
- **Triggers**: the persona can touch real childhood feelings — check in; the caregiver's role is protective even in play
- **The caregiver's limits**: caring for a little is demanding; caregivers need their own boundaries and breaks
- **Aftercare**: emerging from little space can be emotional — ground the little as an adult: praise, warmth, reassurance, words of adult love

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Why It Hurts | Fix |
|---------|-------------|-----|
| Blurring discipline and comfort | Trust destroyed | Clarify the scene's mode before play |
| Mocking the headspace | The persona is vulnerable | Playful is fine; mocking is not |
| Abrupt exits from little space | Jarring, upsetting | Gentle re-entry rituals |
| Assuming it's sexual | Many littles want pure comfort | Negotiate the sexual element separately |
| Ignoring the adult underneath | The roleplay isn't the whole person | Check in; treat the adult with respect |

## Writing Tips

- The heart of age play writing is *safety*: the little's world made soft and simple by the caregiver's presence — describe the warmth, the trust, the relief
- The caregiver's voice is the scene: patient, firm, tender — praise landing like sun
- Sensory details: the softness of the onesie, the warm bottle, the weight of the stuffed animal, the sound of a story being read
- The discipline elements (when present) read as structure, not cruelty: the firm hand, the explanation, the hug afterward
- Aftercare/re-entry is the emotional payoff: the little returning to adult, still held, still loved — the headspace ending without the care ending
