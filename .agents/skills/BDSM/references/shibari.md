# Shibari Guide

This reference covers rope choice, knots, classic ties, floor work, suspension, and safety for the BDSM skill.

## What It Is

Shibari (and its older name, kinbaku) is Japanese rope bondage raised to an art form: the rope is both restraint and aesthetic — patterns drawn on the body, tension that sculpts flesh, a shared meditation between rigger and model. Unlike functional Western bondage, shibari prizes the *journey*: the slow, deliberate wrapping, the communication through tension, the beauty of the finished tie. The rope becomes a conversation; every knot is a word.

## Rope & Equipment

- **Material**: jute (traditional, textured, smells of the fiber) or hemp; cotton (softer, less durable) and synthetic (slick, less traditional) are alternatives
- **Diameter**: 6mm for general use, 8mm for suspension and heavier work
- **Length**: 7–8m per rope; 2–3 ropes (15–25m) is a standard starting set
- **Care**: rope is conditioned, oiled, and retired when it frays; check for splinters and wear before each use
- **Cutting safety**: EMT shears — always within arm's reach, before the first knot

## Core Knots

| Knot | Purpose |
|------|---------|
| **Single-column tie** | The foundation: one rope around one limb — wrists, ankles, thighs |
| **Double-column tie** | Two limbs together — both wrists, wrist-to-ankle |
| **Square knot / lark's head** | Attaching rope to rope, anchors, and structures |
| **Slip knots and quick releases** | Emergency-conscious variations |

## Classic Ties

| Tie | Description |

## Floor Work

Floor work (as opposed to suspension) is where most shibari happens — and it's a complete art in itself:
- **Ground ties** can be as intense as any suspension; restriction, position, and duration do the work
- **Communication through rope**: the rigger reads the model's breathing and tension through the rope; the model signals comfort through micro-movements
- **Time in ties**: 15–20 minutes is plenty for beginners; longer sessions are fine for experienced bodies with monitoring
- **Predicament ties**: tension-based positions where the model must choose between two discomforts — the psychology of the tie

## Suspension (Advanced)

- **Never solo**: suspension requires training, a certified rigger, appropriate hard points, and a spotter — it is genuinely dangerous without all three
- **Partial suspension**: a limb or torso raised — intense but safer than full suspension
- **Full suspension**: the body fully off the ground — requires load-bearing harness ties and knowledge of nerve compression under weight
- **Time limits**: 15–20 minutes max for most full suspensions, even experienced
- **Nerve monitoring**: tingling or numbness in hands/feet = immediate release — no exceptions
- **The floor is lava**: even "brief" suspension risks fainting, nerve damage, and joint strain — no improvisation

## Safety

1. **Nerve map first**: the radial nerve (inner wrist), ulnar (inner elbow), brachial plexus (armpit), peroneal (back of knee), and femoral (inner thigh) are the primary hazards — never place loaded rope directly over them
2. **Two-finger rule** on every column tie
3. **Circulation checks**: color and temperature of hands/feet, every few minutes
4. **Breathing**: chest harnesses must not restrict full expansion — test by having the model take a deep breath mid-tie
5. **Never leave a tied person alone**
6. **Aftercare**: massage rope marks, hydrate, warmth, check skin for rope burns, emotional check-in — rope is intimate and the aftercare matters

## Scene Arc

1. **Preparation** — rope conditioned, shears ready, the model's safeword confirmed
2. **The first rope** — the first wrap is the threshold; the model's breath changes
3. **The conversation** — tension, adjustment, the rigger reading the model
4. **The pattern** — the tie developing into its full form; the model sinking into restriction
5. **Play within the tie** — impact, sensation, worship, orgasm control — the tie as stage
6. **Release** — slow, deliberate unwinding; the skin remembering the rope
7. **Aftercare** — marks massaged, warmth, hydration, connection

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Why It's Dangerous | Fix |
|---------|-------------------|-----|
| Loaded rope over inner wrist/elbow | Nerve compression — tingling, numbness, lasting damage | Learn the nerve map; reposition |
| Too tight | Circulation cut off | Two-finger rule, always |
| Suspension without training | Falls, nerve damage, hard-point failure | Floor work first; certified instruction for suspension |
| Chest harness too tight | Breathing restricted | Deep-breath test mid-tie |
| Rope too close to the neck | Strangulation risk | Never wrap the neck — ever |
| Ignoring tingling | The warning sign IS the injury starting | Release immediately |
| No shears | Emergency can't be answered | Shears in reach, always |
| Untended bottom | Can't self-rescue | Never alone, ever |

## Writing Tips

- Shibari is slow — the eroticism is in the *process*: each wrap, the rope's slide over skin, the model's breath deepening as the tie tightens
- The tension is a conversation: describe the rigger reading the model through the rope, the model communicating without words
- Marks and impressions: the reddened skin where rope crossed, the pattern left on the body like a photograph
- The release is often more intimate than the tying — describe the slow unwinding, the skin's return to freedom
- The aesthetic matters: light, shadow, the geometry of rope against skin — shibari scenes reward visual description
