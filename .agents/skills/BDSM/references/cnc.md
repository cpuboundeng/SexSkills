# Consensual Non-Consent Guide

This reference covers CNC types, the consent structure that makes it possible, safeword mechanics, and safety for the BDSM skill.

## What It Is

Consensual Non-Consent (CNC) is a scene in which the bottom consents in advance to having their apparent refusals ignored. The *appearance* of non-consent is the kink — the bottom's "no" is part of the script — while the reality of consent is more carefully structured than in any other scene. The bottom has already said yes, thoroughly and specifically; during the scene they simply can't say it. CNC is edgeplay: it requires the most trust, the most communication, and the most careful aftercare in BDSM.

## CNC Types

| Type | Description | Intensity |
|------|-------------|-----------|
| **Light CNC** | Ignoring protests during edging or denial ("No, please, I can't —" while the Dom continues) | Easy |
| **Forced position** | Holding a position despite protests and discomfort | Medium |
| **Forced service** | Serving despite refusal; the Dom ignores the no | Medium |
| **Forced sex** | The Dom initiates when the bottom says no — pre-consented | Medium-Hard |
| **Rough CNC** | Pinning, biting, gripping, aggression — with pre-consent | Hard |
| **Full CNC** | A complete scene of apparent coercion; the bottom has no visible power until it ends | Very Hard |
| **Waking CNC** | The bottom half-asleep; the Dom initiates — pre-negotiated | Variable |

## The Consent Structure (What Makes It Safe)

1. **Pre-scene negotiation is exhaustive** — the bottom lists exactly what is allowed: acts, positions, words, intensity, duration; the Dom confirms each
2. **The safeword is the only stop** — distinctive, practiced, non-verbal backup; "no" and "stop" are script, not signals
3. **Limits are absolute** — hard limits are never crossed in CNC, no exceptions; this is not negotiable even in the scene
4. **The bottom stays in control of the *frame*** — they can end the scene at any moment; the power to stop is the power that makes the surrender safe
5. **Visual check-ins without breaking character** — thumbs up/down, a squeeze, an object held and dropped; the Dom checks without speaking
6. **Aftercare is the second half of the scene** — reorientation, reassurance, the explicit reaffirmation that everything was consensual
7. **Debrief follows** — what worked, what felt off, what changes; CNC scenes are reviewed like no others

## Safeword Mechanics

- **Never use "no" or "stop"** as the CNC safeword — they're the script; the safeword must be impossible to confuse with protest ("red," "pineapple," "safeword")
- **Practice the safeword** — bottoms must rehearse saying it so it's available under the intensity of the scene
- **Non-verbal safewords are mandatory**: dropped object, three taps, hand squeeze — the bottom may be unable to speak
- **The Dom must respond to the safeword instantly and completely** — scene over, aftercare starts, no questions until later
- **The bottom can also safeword the *type* of scene**: a yellow that means "too much, change it" rather than ending everything

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Why It's Dangerous | Fix |
|---------|-------------------|-----|
| Using "no" as the safeword | The stop signal gets ignored along with the script | Distinctive safeword, always |
| Skipping negotiation | CNC without boundaries is just assault | Exhaustive pre-negotiation, written if needed |
| Not checking the room | A bottom in genuine distress can look identical to a performing one | Visual check-ins, practiced signals |
| No aftercare plan | The emotional crash of CNC is the hardest in BDSM | Plan aftercare before the scene |
| Drifting from the negotiated script | Doing "a little more" than agreed in CNC is a violation of the core agreement | Stick to the script; renegotiate outside scenes |
| First-time partners | CNC needs established trust and communication history | Build trust over many scenes first |
| Starting with full CNC | Novices misjudging intensity and response | Start light; escalate over time |
| Alcohol or drugs | Judgment and consent are compromised | Sober, always |

## Emotional Reality

- **Real fear happens**: even fully consensual CNC can trigger genuine fear responses — that's part of the intensity, but the Dom must distinguish *arousal-fear* from *distress-fear*
- **The crash is real**: bottoms can feel raw, confused, or shaken after CNC — the line between "acted" and "felt" blurs; aftercare and reorientation are critical
- **Tops can be affected too**: Doms may feel guilt or horror after CNC scenes ("did I hurt them?") — Dom aftercare matters equally
- **Not for everyone**: CNC requires trust, communication skill, and self-knowledge; declining it is wisdom, not weakness

## Scene Arc

1. **Negotiation** — exhaustive: acts, words, limits, duration, safewords, aftercare
2. **The frame** — the scenario set: the "story" of the scene (break-in, punishment, capture)
3. **The scene** — the Dom acts the part; the bottom protests convincingly; the safeword hangs in the air as the real power
4. **The release** — the frame dissolves; the Dom's voice returns to the partner's
5. **Aftercare** — reorientation, warmth, reassurance: "Look at me. It was consensual. You're safe. You did so well."
6. **Debrief** — soon and again later: what was good, what to change, emotional check-ins over the following days

## Writing Tips

- CNC is the most emotionally demanding BDSM fiction — the consent structure is the plot's spine; show it, even briefly ("Red ends everything, remember?" — and mean it)
- The bottom's interiority carries the scene: the conflict between the performed protest and the real arousal, the trust beneath the terror
- The safeword being *honored* is the climax of trust — a scene where the bottom safewords and the Dom stops instantly is the most powerful CNC writing there is
- The aftercare is the emotional payoff: the turn from "scenario partner" back to "cherished person" is the whole meaning of the kink
- Never write CNC without its consent frame — without it, the scene isn't kink, it's horror
