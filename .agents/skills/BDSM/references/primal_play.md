# Primal Play Guide

This reference covers primal headspace, pet play, predator/prey dynamics, and pack dynamics for the BDSM skill.

## What It Is

Primal play strips the scene down to instinct: growling, chasing, wrestling, biting, claiming — the social rules drop away and the body leads. It can be pet play (puppy, kitten, pony — the bottom becomes an animal in a simpler world) or predator/prey (hunt and capture, the rawest power exchange in BDSM). What unites primal play is *headspace*: the shift from thinking to instinct, from language to body.

## Primal Headspace

- **The primal state**: thinking slows, the body takes over — movements become instinctive, sounds replace words
- **For the prey**: the thrill of being hunted, caught, claimed — fear that becomes arousal
- **For the predator**: the drive of the chase, the satisfaction of the catch, the raw ownership of a body caught and held
- **For the pet**: the relief of simplicity — no decisions, no words, just the pack, the routine, the belonging
- **The re-entry**: coming back to language and personhood can take time and care — the aftercare for primal play is partly a return to words

## Pet Play Types

| Type | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **Puppy play** | The most common — eager, playful, loyal; tail-wagging energy |
| **Kitten play** | Sinuous, independent, affectionate on their own terms |
| **Pony play** | Discipline, training, equipment-heavy (bridles, saddles, gait work) |
| **Other animals** | Foxes, bunnies, bears, wolves — the animal fits the person |
| **Handler/human** | The counterpart role: caretaker, trainer, owner |
| **Pack dynamics** | Multiple pets with a hierarchy; or pets with handlers |

### Pet Play Elements
- Collars, leashes, harnesses, muzzles, paws, tails, ears
- Crawling, four-point movement, growing comfortable on the floor
- Feeding from bowls, sleeping in designated spaces (beds, crates, mats)
- Training and commands: sit, stay, heel, fetch
- Non-verbal communication: whines, growls, tail language, nudging
- Praise and pets: head scratches, belly rubs, treats — affection as reward
- **The collar is the switch**: on = pet headspace, off = person

## Predator/Prey Dynamics

| Element | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **The hunt** | The chase — physical or psychological; the prey knowing they're being tracked |
| **The catch** | Capture — pinned, held, claimed; the moment the hunt ends |
| **The claim** | Biting, marking, possessing — the aftermath of the catch |
| **Wrestling** | Physical struggle as foreplay — strength, resistance, surrender |
| **The escape** | Predator/prey scenes may allow escape attempts — the prey runs, gets caught, runs again |
| **The fear-play note** | Real fear can surface — this is edgeplay territory; check-ins and safewords matter as much as in CNC |

## Scene Ideas

- Puppy on a leash, following the handler's commands around the house, then curled at their feet while the handler works
- Kitten being fed from a bowl, petted, then "claimed" by the handler
- Predator/prey in the dark: the prey stalked through the house, caught, and taken against the wall
- Pony training: gait work, equipment, the handler's patient discipline
- Pack scene: multiple pets, a hierarchy, the handler managing the energy
- Wrestling scene: the struggle, the pin, the surrender

## Safety

- **Physical safety**: wrestling and chasing need cleared space, no sharp furniture corners, and awareness of joints
- **Fear is real**: prey play can trigger genuine fear responses — the line between arousal-fear and distress-fear must be watched; safewords are absolute
- **Non-verbal safewords**: a pet who's "not allowed" to speak still needs a signal — a specific sound, a paw tap, a dropped object
- **Headspace management**: deep pet headspace reduces decision-making ability — the handler holds responsibility
- **Ground rules for public**: pet play in public spaces needs agreed boundaries and discretion
- **The floor problem**: crawling and kneeling strain knees and wrists — pads, cushions, breaks
- **Aftercare**: re-entry to personhood — words returning, cuddles, reassurance that they're loved as a person, not just a pet

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Why It's Wrong | Fix |
|---------|---------------|-----|
| Assuming all pet play is sexual | Much of it is comfort and simplicity | Negotiate the sexual element separately |
| Breaking headspace carelessly | Abrupt exits from pet space feel jarring | Gentle re-entry rituals |
| Ignoring fear signals in prey play | Real distress is masked by the game | Distinguish performance from panic |
| Physical escalation without rules | Wrestling without boundaries is injury | Agree intensity, safe words, space |
| No aftercare for the handler | Handlers can feel odd after "owning" someone | Dom aftercare matters too |

## Writing Tips

- Primal writing is sensory and physical: growls, breath, teeth, fur against skin, the weight of a pin, the scratch of nails
- Drop the dialogue — primal scenes communicate in sound and body: the growl that answers a whimper, the hand that lands on a nape
- Interiority for prey: the pounding chase, the instinct to run and the instinct to be caught, the surrender at the catch
- Interiority for pets: the simplicity, the safety, the joy of being an animal in someone's care
- The re-entry is a beautiful scene beat: the animal returning to person, the collar coming off, the words coming back
