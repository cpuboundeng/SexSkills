# Breath Play Guide

This reference covers breath play types, the realities of risk, and safety for the BDSM skill.

## What It Is

Breath play restricts or alters breathing for erotic effect — the lightheaded rush of oxygen shift, the vulnerability of having breath controlled, the primal intimacy of a hand on the throat. It is also **one of the highest-risk activities in BDSM**, responsible for more accidental deaths than almost any other kink. It is edgeplay: the risk cannot be eliminated, only managed. This guide is honest about that.

> **The stakes**: oxygen deprivation can cause unconsciousness within seconds, brain damage within minutes. People have died from "light" breath play. There is no truly safe breath play — only less-risky versions of it, done with informed consent, sober, and supervised.

## Risk Levels (Be Honest With Yourself)

| Type | Risk | Reality Check |
|------|------|---------------|
| **Restricted airflow (mouth/nose covered)** | High | Unconsciousness comes faster than people expect |
| **Throat pressure (choking)** | Very high | The throat carries the airway AND the carotid arteries; pressure on both can drop someone fast |
| **Chest compression (weight/pressure)** | High | Restricts expansion; can crack ribs, strain the heart |
| **Bag play / airtight covering** | **Extreme** | Among the leading causes of kink deaths; a bag over the head can kill in minutes, even with someone watching |
| **Choking at the collar (front pressure on neck)** | High | Compresses the windpipe; panic response is real |
| **Holding breath (self-managed)** | Low-Medium | The least risky form — but still can cause fainting |

**A note on bag play**: plastic bags over the head are responsible for a disproportionate share of BDSM/autoerotic fatalities. They can create an airtight seal, and unconsciousness can occur so quickly that even an attentive partner can't respond in time. This guide does not recommend bag play. If it appears in fiction, it should be written as the extreme edgeplay it is — never casual.

## Safer Approaches (Relative)

If breath play is engaged, the less-risky forms and practices are:
- **Holding breath**: the bottom holds their own breath on command, for a *short, timed* duration — they retain control and the escape valve
- **Mouth covering only, nose free**: a hand over the mouth while the nose stays clear — restricts speech (erotic) without fully blocking air
- **Light collar pressure on the front of the throat**, explicitly *not* on the windpipe — brief, gentle, for the *feeling* of control more than restriction
- **Chest pressure, front only**: kneeling weight on the chest, *never* on the neck — with an immediate off-switch
- **Fiction note**: most written "choking" scenes describe far less pressure than real choking requires — keep fiction to the pressure that controls, not the pressure that closes the airway

## Absolute Rules (If It Happens At All)

1. **Never unattended** — a bottom who goes silent or limp must be seen instantly
2. **No combined risk factors**: no alcohol, no drugs, no exhaustion, no recent head injury, no heart/lung conditions, no pregnancy, no blood pressure issues
3. **Duration is seconds, not minutes** — short, discrete episodes; never sustained
4. **Release early** — the rush peaks before the danger does; stop while it's still good
5. **Watch the signs**: face color (pale or blue = emergency), body going limp, eyes rolling, no response to voice — these are *release and call for help* signals, not "a little further"
6. **No restrictive clothing or harnesses** on the neck/throat during play
7. **Position matters**: never breath play in bondage where the bottom can't shift or signal; never on a bed edge (fainting = falls)
8. **Signals**: the bottom must have a non-verbal stop signal, and the top must be watching for it
9. **Aftercare and watch**: after any breath play, the bottom stays awake, warm, and monitored; headache, confusion, or drowsiness = medical attention
10. **Not for beginners** — to each other or with each other. No "first time" breath play

## What It Feels Like (Fiction)

- **The lightheaded rush**: the room softening at the edges, a floating warmth, sound going distant
- **The control**: the bottom's world narrowing to the hand on their throat, the Dom's eyes, the permission to breathe
- **The return**: air flooding back in — the sharp inhale, the color returning, the body's gratitude for breath
- **The intimacy**: few acts carry the same weight of trust — they are literally handing their breathing to someone

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Why It's Fatal | Fix |
|---------|---------------|-----|
| Bag play "just for a second" | Seconds can be fatal; seal forms fast | Don't do bag play |
| Choking both sides of the neck | Carotid pressure drops someone fast | Front-of-throat light pressure only, windpipe-free |
| Playing after alcohol/drugs | Judgment and reflexes impaired | Sober or nothing |
| Not watching the face | The warning signs are visual | Eyes on the bottom's face the entire time |
| Restraining while doing breath play | Can't signal, can't escape | Restraint and breath play don't mix |
| "They'd tap if they needed to" | Unconsciousness beats the tap | Active monitoring, not passive signals |
| No emergency plan | Every second counts | Know CPR, phone reachable, no hesitation |

## Scene Structure (If It Happens)

Breath play scenes are *short* — the risk lives in duration:

1. **Negotiation** — explicit risk acknowledgment, safewords, medical check, no alcohol
2. **Position** — seated or reclined, never bound, never near a bed edge
3. **The episode** — seconds, not minutes; eyes on the bottom's face throughout
4. **Release early** — while it's still good; the rush peaks before the danger
5. **Recovery** — monitored return to normal breathing, warmth, hydration
6. **Watch period** — the bottom stays awake and observed; no immediate driving or alone time
7. **Aftercare** — the intimacy of air returning, reassurance, emotional check-in

## Writing Tips

- Breath play in fiction is about *power and air*, not anatomy: the hand on the throat is a collar made of the Dom's body
- Describe the rush honestly — the softening edges, the floating, the loud return of breath
- Never write breath play as casually safe; the stakes are what make it hot
- A competent top in fiction checks, releases early, and watches — that competence is the trust the bottom is surrendering to
