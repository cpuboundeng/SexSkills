# Caning Guide

This reference covers cane types, techniques, zones, marks, and safety for the BDSM skill.

## What It Is

Caning uses a thin, flexible rod to deliver focused, precise strikes. The cane is the scalpel of impact play: where a flogger spreads sensation, a cane concentrates it into a clean line of sting. The whistle of the cane through the air, the sharp *crack*, the pause while the sting blooms — caning is impact play at its most deliberate and ceremonial.

## Cane Types

| Type | Description | Sensation |
|------|-------------|-----------|
| **Rattan cane** | Traditional, flexible, durable | Sharp sting with lingering burn |
| **Bamboo cane** | Stiffer, less flexible | Deeper, sharper impact; more thud |
| **Flat cane** | Flattened rattan | Broader sting, less pinpoint |
| **Whip cane** | Thin, whippy, fast | Quick, whip-like sting |
| **Training/thicker cane** | 8mm+ diameter | Deeper thud, more forgiving |
| **Acrylic/Delrin** | Synthetic, smooth | Hard, precise sting |

**For beginners**: a thicker (8mm+) rattan cane — the extra weight gives thud over sharpness and is more forgiving of an unsteady hand. Thin whippy canes bite hard and bruise deeply.

## Techniques

- **The stroke**: a flick of the wrist, not a full swing; the cane accelerates through the target and stops — follow-through determines sting
- **Strike and hold**: keep the cane pressed on the skin a moment — maximizes the sting
- **Strike and draw**: drag the cane across the skin after impact — a line, a longer burn, a dramatic mark
- **Strike through**: carry the stroke through the body — fastest, cleanest, most theatrical
- **Patterning**: lines, grids, crosses, spirals — the skin becomes a canvas; patterned marks are the trophy of a skilled caner
- **Warm-up**: lighter strokes first, letting the skin warm and the bottom settle into the rhythm
- **The pause**: time between strokes is part of the scene — the anticipation, the trembling, the knowledge of what's coming

## Marks

- **Welts**: raised lines that appear within minutes — the cane's signature
- **Lines and patterns**: grid or diagonal patterns fade into a reddened canvas
- **Bruising**: heavy caning can bruise deeply; the same stroke can welt one body and bruise another — start light, learn the bottom's tissue
- **The lingering ache**: caned skin burns for hours, aches for days — a memory that persists

## Safety

- **Warm up first** with hand, paddle, or light strokes
- **Count strokes aloud**; record agreed limits before the scene
- **Max 3–4 consecutive strokes on the same spot**; alternate zones
- **Watch the reaction**: a bottom can go quiet from overload — check in
- **Never strike over fresh marks**; let previous lines settle
- **The cane must not wrap** (wrap-around = the tip striking the side/hip) — aim for the center of the target, watch the follow-through
- **Aftercare**: ice or cool compress, then warmth; the skin is sensitive for days; no re-striking until healed

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Why It's Wrong | Fix |
|---------|---------------|-----|
| Cane wrapping around the body | The tip lands on the side/hip — unintended strikes | Aim center-mass, control follow-through |
| Too many strokes on one spot | Deep bruising, tissue damage | 3–4 max per spot; alternate zones |
| Re-striking fresh welts | Skin damage, extreme pain | Let marks settle between rounds |
| Skipping warm-up | Sharp pain, shock, withdrawal | Hand/paddle warm-up first, always |
| Unagreed stroke counts | The scene runs past limits | Count aloud; agree the number |
| Ignoring quietness | Quiet can be overload, not submission | Check in; read the body |

## Scene Arc

1. **Negotiation** — zones, stroke count, mark tolerance
2. **The ritual** — the cane produced, flexed, tested; the bottom positioned
3. **Warm-up** — lighter strokes; the sound establishing the rhythm
4. **Building** — intensity rising; welts appearing; the sting compounding
5. **The peak** — the hardest strokes; often tears, surrender, or subspace
6. **The afterglow** — the cane set aside; the bottom's burning skin soothed
7. **Aftercare** — marks examined, skin cooled, the bottom grounded

## Writing Tips

- The cane has a voice: the *whistle* before, the *crack* on impact, the bottom's sharp inhale
- Describe the mark appearing — the line of red rising on skin — it's the visible proof of the exchange
- The ritual details are the scene: the Dom flexing the cane, measuring the target, the bottom's trembling anticipation
- Counting is dialogue gold: each number, each "thank you," the arc of the scene in numerals
