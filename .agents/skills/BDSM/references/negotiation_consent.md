# Negotiation & Consent Guide

This reference covers the consent structure that makes every BDSM scene possible — negotiation, limits, safewords, and consent models — for the BDSM skill.

## What It Is

Negotiation is the conversation before the scene in which everyone agrees on what will and won't happen. It's not a mood-killing formality — it's the foundation of trust that makes power exchange possible. In practice and in fiction, the negotiation *is* part of the erotic structure: the sub stating their limits, the Dom promising to hold them, the moment permission is given.

## The Negotiation Conversation

### What to Cover
- **The scene**: what activities, what implements, what intensity range
- **Roles**: who tops, who bottoms, any switching
- **Limits**: hard limits (absolute no-gos) and soft limits (maybe-with-care)
- **Safeword**: the word and the non-verbal signal
- **Check-ins**: how the top will check in, what the bottom will signal
- **Aftercare**: what each person needs afterward, who provides it
- **Health**: injuries, conditions (e.g., circulation issues, recent surgery), medication, pregnancy, emotional state
- **The follow-up**: when and how the scene gets debriefed later

### Hard vs. Soft Limits

| Type | Definition | How It Works |
|------|------------|--------------|
| **Hard limit** | Absolute no — never happens | Stated clearly; never crossed, pressured, or "forgotten" |
| **Soft limit** | Not now, or only with care | Crossed only with fresh, explicit, in-the-moment consent |
| **Limit escalation** | A soft limit becomes a hard limit mid-scene | Any limit can be upgraded to hard at any time — instantly |
| **New territory** | Something never tried before | Treated as a soft limit: slow, small, heavily checked |

## Safewords

The safeword is the one word that always stops the scene. Its power is that it *works* — that's what makes the rest of the scene safe to play with.

### Traffic Light System
- **Green** — "Keep going, more"
- **Yellow** — "Slow down, check in, I'm near a limit" (not a stop; a pause)
- **Red** — "Stop immediately, scene over, aftercare now"

### Non-Verbal Safewords
Needed whenever the bottom can't speak (gagged, breathless, subspace, hooded):
- Dropping a held object (keys, a bell, a ball)
- Tapping a pattern (three taps = stop)
- Hand squeezes (one squeeze = yellow, three = red)
- Head shaking with sound (grunting "no" loudly)
- Thumbs up/down for quick color checks

### Choosing a Safeword
- Pick something that would never come up naturally in the scene — "pineapple," "red," "octopus"
- **Never use "no" or "stop" as the safeword** if the scene involves any play-acting of resistance — in CNC they *must* be different words
- Practice it before the scene so it feels automatic under pressure

## Consent Models

| Model | Stands For | Core Idea |
|-------|------------|-----------|
| **SSC** | Safe, Sane, Consensual | Play should be safe, done sanely, and fully consensual |
| **RACK** | Risk-Aware Consensual Kink | Some kinks carry real risk; the standard is informed consent to known risk |
| **PRICK** | Personal Responsibility, Informed Consensual Kink | Each partner owns their informed choices and their responsibility |

For edgeplay (breath play, heavy impact, CNC, suspension), RACK/PRICK thinking applies: the risk can't be eliminated, so it must be understood, mitigated, and consented to with eyes open.

## Ongoing Consent

Consent isn't a one-time conversation — it's re-established:
- **At the start of each scene** ("Same limits as last time?")
- **When introducing anything new** ("Want to try the cane tonight?")
- **Mid-scene** via check-ins, not just at the end
- **After the scene** in the debrief — consent to *repeat* something is separate from consent to *continue* it

## Renegotiation & Mid-Scene Changes

- Any participant can call yellow/red at any time for any reason — no explanation required in the moment
- A bottom can add a hard limit mid-scene; a top must accept it instantly
- A top can also safeword! Tops can feel overwhelmed, triggered, or uncertain — their safeword matters equally
- Renegotiating mid-scene (adding an activity) requires the bottom to be fully present and verbal — never renegotiate upward while someone is in subspace or distress

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Why It's Dangerous | Fix |
|---------|-------------------|-----|
| Skipping negotiation | Assumptions become boundaries; someone gets hurt | Always negotiate, even with long-term partners |
| "They didn't say no, so it's fine" | Absence of no is not consent | Consent is enthusiastic and explicit |
| Safeword = "no" in a CNC scene | The stop word gets ignored along with the protest | Use a distinctive safeword, always |
| Pressuring past a soft limit | "Just try it" erodes trust permanently | Soft limits stay soft until the bottom raises them |
| No non-verbal safeword when gagged | Bottom can't stop the scene | Always establish a non-verbal signal |
| No aftercare plan | Drop hits unprepared people | Plan aftercare before the scene |
| Not debriefing afterward | Issues never surface, resentments build | Follow up: what worked, what didn't |
| Consent assumed from past scenes | Every scene is a fresh yes | Re-confirm at the start of each new scene |

## In Fiction

The negotiation can be a charged scene of its own: the sub stating limits in a low voice, the Dom repeating them back as a promise, the weight of the moment permission is granted. A single line can anchor an entire scene: *"Red stops everything. You remember what red is?"* — and the sub nodding, already breathless. Showing the safeword exist and be honored is what makes everything else you write feel safe enough to be hot.
