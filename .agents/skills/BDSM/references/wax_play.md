# Wax Play Guide

This reference covers candle types, temperature, techniques, zones, removal, and safety for the BDSM skill.

## What It Is

Wax play drips melted candle wax onto skin — a slow, intimate, sensual cruelty. The heat lands, spreads, cools, and hardens, leaving a trail of warmth that lingers on the skin. It pairs beautifully with bondage (a bound body can't escape the drips), sensation contrast (ice on the cooling wax), and impact play (wax struck while soft). The anticipation of the next drip — the bottom never knows exactly where it will land — is the heart of the practice.

## Candle Types

| Type | Melting Point | Sensation | Notes |
|------|---------------|-----------|-------|
| **Body-safe soy** | ~120–130°F (49–54°C) | Warm, gentle | The beginner's standard; most forgiving |
| **Beeswax** | ~145°F (63°C) | Warmer, slightly stickier | Medium intensity; used from a distance |
| **Specialty wax play candles** | Labeled for skin | Formulated for purpose | Check the label; trusted brand |
| **Low-temp wax** | ~110–120°F (43–49°C) | Very gentle | Sensitive skin, beginners, nipples |
| **Paraffin household candles** | Variable, often too hot | **Unsafe for skin** | Avoid — household paraffin can burn unpredictably; many brands melt too hot or spit |

**Rule**: use candles labeled for body/skin use (soy, beeswax, or specialty). Test every candle on your own inner wrist before it touches anyone.

## Techniques

| Technique | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Dripping** | Straight drips from height; height cools the wax — higher = cooler on skin |
| **Swirling** | Tilted candle, circular motion — smooth warm sweeps |
| **Pooling** | Wax collecting in one spot — concentrated warmth |
| **Feathering** | A brush or finger dragging the wax — light, linear |
| **Stringing** | Pulling cooled wax into thin strands — visual and tactile |
| **Ice contrast** | Ice after wax — the cooling hardens and sharpens |
| **Impact contrast** | Wax dripped, then struck while soft — double sensation |
| **Wax + worship** | Wax on the body, then the wax licked or kissed off — intimate |

## Removal

- **Wait for the wax to cool and harden** — peeling hot wax burns
- **Don't rip it off** — peel slowly from an edge, or warm it with a warm hand first
- **Oil is the trick**: baby oil, coconut oil, or massage oil dissolves wax residue beautifully
- **Warm wash afterward** — the skin under wax is often flushed and sensitive

## Safety

- **Test temperature** on your own wrist first — every candle, every session
- **Body-safe candles only**; never household paraffin
- **Height controls heat** — drip from higher for cooler wax; test the fall
- **Keep water, cloth, and matches away from the bottom** — open flame is a hazard near skin, hair, and fabrics
- **Blow out candles immediately if anything changes** — panicked bottom, spilled wax, singed hair
- **Wax burns are real** — white spots, blistering, or pain that persists = stop and treat as a burn
- **Skin check after**: some bodies react to wax (redness, irritation); note which candles agree with which skin
- **Aftercare**: gentle wash, oil for residue, moisturizer; the skin stays sensitive for hours

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Why It's Wrong | Fix |
|---------|---------------|-----|
| Household candles on skin | Unpredictable burns | Body-safe soy/beeswax/specialty only |
| Wax on hairy areas | Painful hair pulling | Trim or avoid; warn the bottom |
| Peeling too early | Hot wax under hardened shell | Wait for full hardening; warm before peeling |
| Testing on the bottom first | Burns if too hot | Test on your own wrist, every candle |
| Open flame near fabric/hair | Fire hazard | Water and cloth ready; clear space |
| Wax on broken skin or fresh marks | Irritation, infection | Check skin first |

## Scene Arc

1. **Setup** — candles lit, water nearby, the bottom positioned (bound works beautifully)
2. **The first drip** — the bottom's body learning the heat; the anticipation begins
3. **Building** — patterns, pools, contrast; the wax trail spreading
4. **The peak** — the most concentrated warmth or the cruellest contrast
5. **Cooling** — the wax hardening; the sensation lingering
6. **Removal** — peeling as its own ritual — sometimes erotic, sometimes tender
7. **Aftercare** — oil, wash, skin check, warmth

## Writing Tips

- The drip is the unit of suspense: *where* will it land, *how* hot, *when* — the bottom's body braced, then the warmth blooming
- Describe the stages: the liquid heat, the spread, the cool that follows — wax is a full sensory arc in itself
- The hardened wax on skin is a visual gift: the white trails against flushed skin, the texture of it cracking when the body moves
- Removal is an underrated scene moment: the slow peel, the skin's sensitivity beneath, the Dom's care in doing it gently
