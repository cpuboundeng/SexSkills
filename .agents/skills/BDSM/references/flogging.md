# Flogging Guide

This reference covers flogger types, anatomy, techniques, zones, and safety for the BDSM skill.

## What It Is

Flogging uses a multi-tail implement (a handle with many falling strands) to deliver a broad wave of sensation — the opposite of the cane's pinpoint precision. A good flogger wraps sensation across the skin: the *thud* of a heavy leather, the whisper of silk, the crackle of the falls landing together. Flogging can be rhythmic, almost musical — the flogger becomes an instrument and the bottom's skin the drum.

## Flogger Types

| Type | Material | Sensation | Best For |
|------|----------|-----------|----------|
| **Leather** | Heavy leather falls | Deep thud with sting | Full impact sessions |
| **Suede** | Soft suede | Smooth, warm thud | Beginners, warm-up |
| **Silk/Satin** | Silk strands | Light, teasing whisper | Teasing, sensory play |
| **Rope** | Braided rope falls | Textured, scratchy sting | Sensory variety |
| **Rubber/vinyl** | Rubber falls | Sharp, biting sting | Intense play |
| **Chain** | Metal | Cold, sharp sting | Experienced; dramatic |
| **Hybrid** | Mixed materials | Mixed sensations | Experienced players |

## Flogger Anatomy

- **Handle**: weight and grip affect control — a heavy handle delivers more force, a light one more speed
- **Falls**: the tails. More falls = wider, softer impact; fewer = sharper. Longer falls = more reach and more snap
- **Heads**: the tips of the falls — the part that makes contact. Different materials give thud or sting
- **The flogger's sound**: the whistle of falls, the thud of leather, the crackle of rubber — each flogger has a voice

## Techniques

- **Warm-up**: silk or suede, slow, gentle — the bottom learns the flogger's voice before its weight
- **Flat strikes**: falls land flat across the surface — broad, even thud
- **Tip work**: only the tips make contact — sharper, more focused
- **Stacking**: repeated strikes on the same area, building heat in a small zone
- **Rhythm play**: alternating fast and slow — the anticipation is the point
- **The flogger's arc**: full overhead swings deliver the deepest thud; wrist flicks deliver speed and sting
- **Sensory contrast**: flogger, then feather, then ice — the cruelty of the contrast

## Safety

- **Warm up always** before heavier impact
- **Inspect the flogger**: falls securely attached, no loose heads, no sharp edges
- **Same spot rule**: 3–4 strikes max consecutively on one area
- **Never the neck, spine, kidneys, joints, or face**
- **Heavy flogging leaves marks for days** — confirm the bottom is okay with visible marks
- **Watch the bottom**: catching breath, tension, quiet — check in
- **Aftercare**: cool compress, aloe or moisturizer, gentle massage, warmth

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Why It's Wrong | Fix |
|---------|---------------|-----|
| Striking the neck or spine | Serious injury risk | Neck and spine are off-limits, always |
| Falls not secured | A loose fall becomes a whip | Inspect the flogger before play |
| Same spot repeatedly | Overheating, bruising | 3–4 strikes max per spot |
| Too heavy too soon | Shock, withdrawal, injury | Warm up with silk/suede, always |
| Ignoring the bottom's quiet | Overload can silence | Check in; watch breath and tension |
| Marks without warning | Real-world consequences | Confirm mark tolerance before play |

## Scene Arc

1. **Preparation** — the flogger chosen and tested; the bottom positioned
2. **Warm-up** — silk or suede strokes; the rhythm established
3. **Building** — heavier falls, faster rhythm, the bottom's body warming
4. **The waves** — intensity in waves: build, settle, build higher
5. **The peak** — sustained impact; the bottom's surrender
6. **Cool-down** — light strokes, touch, warmth
7. **Aftercare** — the reddened skin soothed; the bottom grounded

## Writing Tips

- The flogger's sound is the scene's soundtrack: the whisper of silk, the *thwack* of leather, the crackle of rubber
- Describe sensation as a wave — the flogger covers ground a cane can't; the whole back lights up at once
- Rhythm is character: the Dom's steady, building cadence tells the reader exactly where the scene is going
- The aftermath: reddened, glowing skin; the bottom's whole back burning warm — sensation they can feel for hours
