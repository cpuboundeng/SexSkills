# Sensation Play Guide

This reference covers temperature, texture, pinching, electroplay, and needle play for the BDSM skill.

## What It Is

Sensation play is impact play's gentler cousin and its secret weapon: instead of force, it uses *contrast and texture* to flood the nervous system. Ice, heat, feathers, fur, bristles, vibration, pinching — the game is mixing and alternating sensations to keep the bottom's body in a state of delicious uncertainty. Sensation play works beautifully alone and even better layered over other scenes: the feather after the cane, the ice after the flogger.

## Temperature Play

| Sensation | How It Works | Notes |
|-----------|-------------|-------|
| **Ice** | Slid over skin, held against erogenous zones, dripped as meltwater | Numbs briefly, shocks awake; the contrast afterward is electric |
| **Warm wax** | Dripped from body-safe candles | Slow, spreading warmth — see `wax_play.md` |
| **Hot oil** | Warmed oil poured and spread | Gliding heat, massage adjacent |
| **Heat packs / warm hands** | After cold | The *return* of warmth after ice is a sensation of its own |
| **Cold/warm contrast** | Alternating | The nervous system's favorite trick |

**The rule of contrast**: the best sensation play is *paired* — soft after rough, cold after hot, gentle after intense. The body feels the second sensation twice as hard because of the first.

## Pinching & Wringing

- **Pinching**: skin rolls between fingers — from teasing tweaks to fierce holds
- **Nipple play**: pinching, twisting, rolling between fingers, clamps (start with gentle clothespins, graduate to purpose-made clamps; set timers)
- **Ear pinching**: classic Dom gesture — symbolic and sensitive
- **Wringing**: twisting skin and tissue — deeper, more intense than pinching
- **Clamps**: nipple, lip, labia, and skin clamps create sustained pressure; removal is its own sensation (the *release* rush) — remove slowly or quickly per preference, and never leave on longer than negotiated

## Electroplay

Low-voltage electrical sensation — modern toys make this accessible and safe when rules are followed:
- **TENS units**: medical-style pulsing pads; tingle to strong buzz
- **Violet wands**: crackling static-like touch; sharp, surprising
- **E-stim toys**: purpose-made BDSM electrical toys with multiple channels
- **Vibrators on skin**: the gateway — buzzing on any body part, not just erogenous zones

**Electroplay safety (strict)**:
- **Never across the chest/heart** — current across the heart is a genuine cardiac risk
- **Never on the neck, head, or face**
- **Never in or near water** — no baths, no wet play
- **No metal jewelry** in the current path
- **Start at zero and dial up** — perception varies wildly between bodies
- **Check for pacemakers/medical devices** — electrical play is off-limits
- **Learn your device** — read the manual; practice on your own forearm first
- **Never leave a clamped/pad wearer unattended**

## Needle Play

Surface needle play (sterile, single-use needles threaded through skin folds for temporary piercings) is an intense, meditative practice — **not for beginners**:
- Requires medical-grade supplies, sterile technique, proper disposal, and ideally instruction from an experienced mentor
- It's about the ritual and the altered state more than the pain
- Never reuse needles; never work over joints, the face, or near arteries; have a first-aid plan
- If you're writing it, keep it to characters with established experience and negotiation

## Scene Arc

1. **Warm the body** — touch, massage, light sensation to open the nervous system
2. **The first contrast** — a single sensation introduced (feather, ice, heat)
3. **Layering** — alternating and stacking sensations; the bottom's body learning the game
4. **The peak** — the strongest contrast or the most sustained tease
5. **Cool-down** — soft, warm, grounding touch
6. **Aftercare** — skin check (ice/wax/clamps leave their marks), warmth, hydration

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Why It's Wrong | Fix |
|---------|---------------|-----|
| All sting, no relief | Sensation play needs contrast | Pair every intense sensation with its opposite |
| Ice held too long | Frostbite/numbing danger | Keep ice moving; no prolonged contact |
| Clamps left on too long | Tissue damage | Timers; negotiated duration |
| Electro across the chest | Cardiac risk | Head/chest/neck are always off-limits |
| Wax from household candles | Burns | Body-safe wax only — see `wax_play.md` |
| Bristles/scratch on broken skin | Infection, pain | Check skin first; avoid fresh marks |
| Overwhelming the bottom | Sensory overload isn't always pleasure | Check in; build slowly |

## Writing Tips

- Sensation play is where sensory language shines: the *shock* of ice, the *whisper* of a feather, the *bloom* of heat returning
- The bottom's body tells the story: goosebumps, shivers, breath hitching, skin flushing — describe the nervous system, not just the touch
- Contrast is the plot: the ice that makes the next touch unbearable, the feather that follows the cane
- Blindfolded sensation play writes beautifully — the reader knows what's coming even when the character doesn't
