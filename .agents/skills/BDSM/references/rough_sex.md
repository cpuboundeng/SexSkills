# Rough Sex Guide

This reference covers rough sex techniques, zones, and safe intensity for the BDSM skill.

## What It Is

Rough sex brings BDSM's intensity into intercourse itself: harder, faster, more forceful — gripping, pinning, hair-pulling, neck-holding, spanking mid-thrust. The line between "rough" and "hurt" is the entire art: rough sex is about *intensity and dominance*, never about actually injuring. The consent and connection that make it hot are the same ones that keep it safe — and in fiction, the difference between rough and abusive is written into the details: the check-in, the read of the body, the aftercare.

## Techniques

- **Grip marks**: deliberate fingerprints on thighs, hips, waist, ass — the body marked by the hands
- **Neck control**: hand on the throat (pressure, not airway), chin gripping, head held in place
- **Hair control**: yanking to direct the face, tugging mid-kiss, pulling the head back to expose the throat
- **Forceful positioning**: flipping, pushing against walls, pinning onto the bed, rearranging at will
- **Deep thrusting**: driving to the hilt with force and rhythm; the pace as an assertion of ownership
- **Edge of pain**: riding the line between pleasure and pain during penetration
- **The verbal layer**: commands and praise — "Take it" — "That's it, you're mine" — the words doing as much as the force
- **The contrast trick**: rough intensity punctuated by a sudden soft touch — the gentleness after force lands harder

## Safety

- **Neck pressure is light**: never block the airway; never compress both sides of the neck; thumb on the windpipe is dangerous — see `breath_play.md`
- **Marks need consent**: grip marks and bruises have real-world consequences (work, family) — ask about mark tolerance before and during
- **Communication**: partners often go quiet during intense sex — agree on signals (a tap, a word, a squeeze) that work mid-scene
- **Positions**: don't pin joints at painful angles; watch for "wrong pain" (sharp vs. good ache)
- **Rough ≠ actually hurting**: the goal is intensity within the partner's limits; pain that's the *point* is negotiated BDSM, not rough sex
- **Check-in after**: ask what was good, what was too much; adjust next time
- **Aftercare**: marked skin checked, soreness tended, the emotional landing — see the `aftercare` skill

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Why It's Wrong | Fix |
|---------|---------------|-----|
| Real choking during rough sex | Airway compromise is an emergency | Light pressure only; breath play rules apply |
| Marks without consent | Real-world consequences | Ask about marks, always |
| Ignoring a partner's stillness | Quiet can be distress, not enjoyment | Signals and check-ins |
| Escalating "in the moment" | Momentum isn't consent | Pre-agree the intensity range |
| No aftercare | The contrast crashes | Tend the marks and the emotions |

## Scene Arc

1. **The opening move** — the first roughness: the grip, the hair yank, the pin
2. **Escalation** — intensity building through force, speed, and words
3. **The peak** — the hardest, deepest, most forceful passage
4. **The turn** — a sudden gentleness: the soft touch after force, the kiss after the grip
5. **The finish** — release, collapse, the held moment
6. **Aftercare** — marks traced, the check-in, the landing — see the `aftercare` skill

## Writing Tips

- The heat of rough sex writing is in the *verbs*: grip, yank, pin, drive, take — and the contrast of the soft moment that follows
- Interiority sells it: the receiver's mind racing between "too much" and "don't stop" — the edge they're riding is the story
- The Dom's control is the turn-on: force *with* precision, strength *with* care — the grip that could hurt choosing not to
- Aftercare is the proof: the marked skin being traced, the "you okay?" that means it — see the `aftercare` skill
- Keep the consent visible: a pre-scene look, a mid-scene check, the afterglow of trust — it's the difference between rough and wrong
