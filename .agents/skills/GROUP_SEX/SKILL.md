## Writing Tips

### The Geometry Problem

Establish the layout. Who is where, who is touching whom, who is watching whom.

> *"Two knees parted her thighs. A third hand traced her ribs from above. Marcus entered from behind; Jake knelt at her head. Four bodies, two angles, one center."*

### Sensory Checklist

- **Touch:** Multiple hands, skin sliding, heat of extra bodies, weight of multiple breaths
- **Sound:** Layered breathing, skin slapping at different rhythms, partner-to-partner sounds
- **Sight:** Who looks at whom, body geometry, stillness vs. motion contrast
- **Smell/Taste:** Multiple scents mingling, the taste of one partner shared between mouths
- **Spatial:** Being surrounded, pressure of bodies in close quarters

### Vocabulary

**Positioning:** flank, bracket, sandwich, straddle, kneel between, stack, chain, circle, rotate, converge, orbit

**Simultaneous:** concurrently, at once, in concert, in tandem, double, layered, compounding

**Receiver sensation:** overwhelmed, enveloped, filled from two directions, split attention, cascading

**Giver sensation:** watching, sharing, competing for, complementing, mirroring, alternating

### Scene Pacing

1. **The assembly** — Map the geometry
2. **The first connection** — Foreplay to sex transition
3. **The pattern** — How the group organizes: center, chain, or independent pairs
4. **Escalation** — New positions, added bodies, faster rhythm
5. **The peak** — Maintain the position that works
6. **The wind-down** — Lower intensity, aftercare

### Common Mistakes

1. **Lost reader:** Too many names, no geometry. Use spatial language to orient.
2. **Parallel play trap:** Two independent couples in the same room. Create cross-connections.
3. **The orphan:** One person left out. Assign an active task early.
4. **Speed mismatch:** One full tilt, another barely warmed up. Sync the pace.
