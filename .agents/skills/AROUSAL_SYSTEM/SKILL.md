# AROUSAL SYSTEM

## Overview

The arousal system is a pacing architecture for sex scenes built on one idea: **arousal is two meters, not one.** Every character runs a *physical* meter (what the body feels and can do) and a *mental* meter (what the mind wants and why). The two rise at different rates, respond to different stimuli, and only *together* do they produce an orgasm that lands.

Most rushed scenes share a single failure: the writer escalates physical acts — kiss, touch, bare skin, hand, mouth, penetration — because each act is an easy "next thing." Every rung of that ladder is a *physical* event, so the body meter races to its ceiling and the scene must climax long before it should, or stall with a fake interruption. The dual-track system fixes this by giving the writer a second dial. Physical acts alone can't end the scene, because an orgasm needs the *mind* to arrive too — and the mind is fueled by everything the body is not: words, waiting, meaning, power, vulnerability, the charged gap between wanting and having.

**This is a scene-architecture skill, not an act skill.** It layers over `foreplay`, `sex`, `climax`, and any act guide. It is also **not** BDSM authority structure: orgasm *control* as a power dynamic (who is allowed to come, under whose orders) is a kink, covered in `bdsm/references/orgasm_control.md`. The arousal system is the neutral craft engine underneath — usable in any scene, vanilla or kinky, and especially in long-form RP where pacing must survive many turns.

**The scaffolding is off-page.** The meters are the writer's instruments, not the character's vocabulary — nobody in the story says "my physical arousal is an eight." See [Tells & Writing](#tells--writing) for how the numbers surface in prose.

## The Failure Mode: The Fuse Ladder

Watch what happens when a scene is built only from physical escalation:

> *They kiss in the doorway. His hands slide under her shirt. Her bra comes off, his belt hits the floor. His mouth finds her, her hand finds him, she gasps against the mattress — and two paragraphs later she's already cresting, and the reader thinks: that's it?*

Every beat above is a **P-event**: it drives the *physical* meter. A chain of P-events is a fuse — it burns in one direction at one speed. The writer who only knows how to escalate physically has exactly two options once the fuse reaches the end: deliver the orgasm (too early) or yank the scene off-course (doorbell, interruption, sudden "not yet" with no reason behind it). Both deflate.

**The fix is not to escalate more slowly. It is to escalate on two tracks.** Physical acts advance the body; meaning, tension, words, and waiting advance the mind. A scene built on alternating tracks can run far longer than one built on a single ladder — not because the physical action is stretched thin, but because the story between physical acts is doing real work.

## The Two Tracks

### P — Physical arousal (the body)
What the body registers and can do: wetness, erection, sensitivity, flush, heartbeat, breath, muscle tension, the involuntary responses (hips moving, thighs pressing, sounds escaping). P is honest and literal — it responds to *touch*. It is the meter that orgasm ultimately needs: a body at its physical ceiling is *ready to come*.

| Band | P | What the body is doing |
|---|---|---|
| Cold | 0–2 | Baseline. Breath even, skin neutral, no focus. |
| Stirred | 3–4 | Pulse up, skin warm, early signals — the first hint of wetness/hardening, a flush starting. |
| Aroused | 5–6 | Fully responsive: obvious wetness/erection, nipples/breath sensitive, touch registers *strongly*. |
| High | 7–8 | On the climb: body is demanding, rhythmic sensation builds fast, muscles begin to tense, the involuntary sounds start. |
| Near-edge | 9 | Everything tightens toward the crest; sustained direct stimulation peaks within moments. |
| Threshold | 10 | Point of no return — the body *will* come with the next crest of stimulation. |

### M — Mental arousal (the mind)
What the mind is doing: desire, anticipation, fixation, fantasy, vulnerability, meaning — the charge of *who* is touching and *why*. M is what makes an orgasm matter. It responds to *gap and meaning*, not touch: distance, delay, words, eye contact, risk, taboo, novelty, history, power, being seen. M can be enormous while the body sits nearly untouched — and it can be flat while the body is being thoroughly worked.

| Band | M | What the mind is doing |
|---|---|---|
| Absent | 0–2 | Not thinking about sex; or thinking about it without wanting it. |
| Noticing | 3–4 | Attention keeps returning; noticing the other body, the moment, the possibility. |
| Wanting | 5–6 | Clear desire, fixated on the *idea*; actively imagining, anticipating, choosing. |
| Hungry | 7–8 | Craving: the mind is narrating what it wants, bargaining, barely patient. |
| Overrun | 9 | Thought collapses into need — focus narrows to the other person, planning stops. |
| Flooded | 10 | Full surrender of attention; nothing else exists; the wanting *is* the thought. |

### The three laws of the meters

1. **The meters are independent.** A body can be at 8 while the mind sits at 3 (mechanical, hollow, or a body responding against a reluctant mind). A mind can be at 9 while the body idles at 2 (the tease, the look across the room). Independence is the steering wheel — it is what gives you somewhere to steer.
2. **P answers to intensity; M answers to gap and meaning.** More friction, pressure, and rhythm raises P. More distance between *want* and *satisfaction* — plus the meaning of who, why, and what's at stake — raises M. This asymmetry is the whole system: it means the fastest way to heat a scene is not always to touch more.
3. **Orgasm needs P at threshold, but needs M to *mean*.** (See The Orgasm Gate below.) This is a craft law, not a biology textbook — people absolutely come with flat minds. The system treats that as a *deliberate setting*, not the default.

## The Orgasm Gate

Orgasm requires two conditions:

- **P ≥ 10** — the body is at its physical threshold (physiologically "ready"), **and**
- **M ≥ the gate** — the mind is engaged enough for the orgasm to *land* as the scene intends.

The gate is not a fixed number; it's a dial you set per scene and per character:

| Scene intent | Gate setting | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Casual / quickie / purely physical | M 3–4 | A release can be just a release. The *heat* is the point, not the meaning. |
| Standard earned scene | M 7 | The default: climax reads as satisfying when the mind is truly along. |
| Emotional / first-time / reunion / confession | M 9–10 | The peak only lands when the character is emotionally wrecked first. |
| Deliberately hollow (post-fight, grief-sex, detachment) | Break the gate on purpose | The body crests while the mind watches — the *emptiness* becomes the beat. |

**What the gate buys you:** when P crosses 10 but M sits below the gate, the scene does not *have* to climax — the character is right at the edge but can't crest (the **false peak**), or crests hollow, or gets pulled back. That single rule converts "I don't want them to come yet" from a problem into a *choice*: you simply keep M below the gate until you're ready, and spend the intervening time raising M with everything that isn't touch. When P is at 9–10 and M is at the gate, *that* is your chosen moment — the scene's point of no return — and you write the approach (see the `climax` skill).

**Two honest edge cases:**
- **Gate-broken climaxes must be visible.** If you climax a character with M low (the hollow setting), the prose must show the emptiness — the crest that happens *to* the body while the mind stays cold — or the reader will assume satisfaction and the arc lands wrong.
- **Body response ≠ consent.** The P-high/M-low state is precisely the state in which a body responds while a mind refuses. When you write that state in a reluctant, CNC-adjacent, or dark context, keep the character's *actual* state legible on the page. A body's response is not agreement; if the scene means "this is happening against her will," do not let the prose quietly imply her body's response makes it okay.

## What Raises What: The Rate Table

Typical deltas per beat for a moderately responsive character. These are **ratios, not arithmetic** — the same act raises P less on the fifth repetition (familiarity dulls the body), and M varies wildly with *who* and *why*. Use the table as a sense of direction, not a calculator.

| Beat | P | M | System note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Eye contact held a beat too long | ~ | ↑↑ | The cheapest M-raiser in fiction. Zero risk of rushing. |
| Words — asking, praising, narrating want | ~–↑ | ↑↑ | Voice is the fastest M-delivery system there is. |
| Memory / fantasy spoken aloud | ↑ | ↑↑ | Past and future tense both charge the present. |
| Kiss (mouth) | ↑ | ↑ | The classic hinge: moves both, moves neither fast. |
| Kiss deep, bodies pressed | ↑↑ | ↑ | Friction enters; clothes are the governor. |
| Clothed touch / grinding | ↑↑ | ↑ | Heat without direct stimulation — safe ramp. |
| Skin bared, mouth on neck/chest | ↑↑ | ↑ | Direct contact begins; watch the climb. |
| Hand job / fingering | ↑↑ | ↑ | Sustained rhythm edges fast — vary it. |
| Oral | ↑↑↑ | ↑↑ | Steepest P-rise on the table; also an M gift for the giver (the view, the trust). |
| Penetration, steady rhythm | ↑↑↑ | ↑ | The classic premature-climax zone — never park here unchaperoned. |
| Zone switch away from target | ↓ | ↑ | Mouth leaves the clit for the thigh; hand leaves the cock for the hip. The tease. |
| Withdrawal / hover | ↓ | ↑↑ | Stop contact, stay close, breathe on the skin. Gap is M-fuel. |
| Pause to talk / check in | ↓ | ↑ | Words cool the body and heat the mind simultaneously. |
| A meaning-bomb ("I love you", the unsaid thing, a confession) | ~ | ↑↑↑ | The heaviest M-raiser there is. Use once, aim it. |
| Power move / order / submission | ↑ | ↑↑ | Authority lands on both meters, differently per character. |
| Vulnerability shown (shaking hands, admitting need, asking) | ~ | ↑↑ | Nothing raises M like the cost of the admission. |
| Tease / denial ("not yet", pulled back at the brink) | ↓↓ | ↑↑ | Frustration spikes M while P cools — the core anti-rush move. |
| External interruption (doorbell, phone) | ↓↓ | ↓↓ | Kills both. Use almost never; see Goalpost Moves. |
| The drive home in silence, hand on the thigh | ↓–~ | ↑↑ | Travel is underrated M-territory. |
| Risk / new context (almost caught, new place) | ↑ | ↑↑ | Novelty and stakes spike both; keep it honest. |

**The context column is half the table.** A kiss raises P and M differently depending on whether it's a first kiss, a goodbye kiss, or a kiss after a year apart. When in doubt, ask *what is this beat worth to this character* — the answer tells you which meter to move.

## The Pacing Rules (the anti-rush laws)

### 1. The Interleave Rule
Every physical act that moves P significantly must be preceded or followed by a beat that moves M — an exchange of words, a held look, a memory, a decision, a moment of vulnerability. **A scene rushes when P-events are strung together without M-beats between them.** (Deliberate mechanical sex breaks this rule on purpose — but then the *mechanicalness* is the point, and it must be visible.)

### 2. The Leash Rule
Don't let P run more than ~3 points ahead of M for more than a beat or two. When the body outruns the mind, the reader senses a body without a heart — it reads as hollow, going-through-the-motions, or *not ready*. If P is outrunning M:
- lift M (words, meaning, eye contact), **or**
- drop P (zone switch, pace change, a pause), **or**
- use the gap deliberately (see The Four States).

### 3. The Plateau Rule
Arousal is not a switch. Between P 7 and P 9 the body can *hover* — held, aching, right at the lip of the edge — without cresting, as long as stimulation is varied rather than relentlessly direct. The plateau is the scene's most flexible zone: you can raise M there without spending P, hold both and let dialogue and dynamics cook, or swap who's in control. **Writing the plateau is writing the holding** — the breath caught, the hips kept still, the "don't move." If a character is at P 9 with steady direct stimulation, they *will* come; plateaus live in variation, not in willpower alone. (For the power-dynamic version of the plateau — held at the edge by command — see `bdsm/references/orgasm_control.md`.)

### 4. The Budget Rule & the Goalpost Move
Decide before the scene where the first orgasm lands: roughly which act, at which M. Then build so P and M *arrive together* at that moment. If they arrive early, you have two choices — and one of them is not "slow down the body." It is to **move the goalpost**: change what the characters want mid-scene, raise the stakes, add a complication, so "enough" is redefined at a higher altitude.

> *They're against the wall, both nearly gone, a minute from the gate. Instead of a doorbell, she stills his hand and says, breathless, "Not here. Take me home."*

The "not here / not yet / I want you to watch / I want more" line is a **character-driven goalpost move** — the character chooses to redefine the destination, so the tension relocates rather than dies: the car ride becomes the tease, the hallway becomes the prelude, the undressing becomes the main course. This is why good long scenes don't feel padded: the physical escalation keeps rising, but what "enough" means keeps climbing ahead of it.

**Rule of thumb:** prefer character-driven withholding ("not yet," a new want, a condition) to plot-driven interruption (the phone, the knock). Interruptions kill both meters; a goalpost move cools P while *raising* M.

## Raising M: The Mental Toolbox

When the body is ahead and you need the mind to catch up, reach for anything that feeds the *gap* or the *meaning*:

- **Announcement and deferral.** The promise made and not yet kept: "later," "when we get home," the look at the clock, the text sent beforehand. The gap between the promise and its delivery is pure M.
- **The gaze.** Being watched wanting, and watching wanting. Eye contact held, the slow undressing look, catching the other staring. *Being seen* is one of the highest-M states in fiction.
- **Words.** Dirty talk, praise, questions ("tell me what you want"), narration of desire, a command or a plea. Voice carries desire straight into the mind — see the `verbal-erotica` skill.
- **Power and its exchange.** Orders, obedience, begging, being given permission, being denied. Authority and surrender both feed M — through different doors, depending on the character.
- **Vulnerability.** The admission with a cost: "I've thought about this every night," the shaking hands, the ask. M rises fastest when the character risks something to want.
- **History and memory.** Callbacks — a previous almost, a shared joke, "you did this the first time." The past is already charged; spend it.
- **Fantasy spoken or shown.** "I imagined you like this," the described future, the thing they'll do later.
- **Context and risk.** Taboo, novelty, the wrong place, the possibility of being seen, the reason this is forbidden or important. Stakes are M.
- **Meaning.** Why this matters — reunion, first time, make-up, goodbye, the declaration. The one thing the scene is *really* about, named or not.

**The M writer's secret:** M beats are almost always cheaper than P beats — a sentence of eye contact or a line of dialogue raises M more than a paragraph of friction raises P. When a scene needs length, write the M.

## Holding and Dialing P: The Physical Toolbox

When the mind is catching up — or the body is climbing too fast — these are the dials. Each one cools or holds P *without* cooling the scene, because each one feeds M on the way down:

- **The zone switch.** Move off the target: the mouth leaves the clit to drag across the inner thigh; the hand leaves the cock to grip the hip, trace the stomach, hold the jaw. Secondary zones are P-coolers and M-heaters at once.
- **The pace change.** Slow to near-still, lighten to a whisper of touch, change the rhythm. Constant intensity is what *peaks* a body; variation is what sustains it. A character held in a slow, shallow rhythm can hover at 9 for a very long time.
- **The withdrawal and the hover.** Stop contact, stay close, breathe on the skin, let the wanting sit in the space. The most powerful dial in the kit, and the cheapest to write.
- **The act substitution.** Swap the P-event for an M-event: penetration or oral gives way to kissing and talk, grinding through clothes, hands that wander without landing.
- **The verbal governor.** "Not yet," "wait," "look at me," "don't come until I tell you." A command converts an involuntary rush into a controlled one — and hands the pacing to whoever speaks. (In power dynamics, the *receiver* begging is often the heat — see `bdsm/references/orgasm_control.md`.)
- **The physical redirect.** Reposition, move to another surface, stand up, change the angle, carry them somewhere. The interrupt that resets rhythm without resetting heat.
- **Temperature and texture.** Cool breath on hot skin, ice, silk, a change of surface. Novelty spikes attention and derails the automatic climb.
- **The environment.** The cold air outside, the walk, the drive. Travel cools the body while the mind reheats — one of the best free dials in long scenes.

**When not to dial P:** quickies, angry sex, reunion sex, and rough scenes often *want* the fuse. Fast-burn mode is a legitimate setting — the system's rules relax when the scene's point is speed: start P high (context has already done the work — they've been wanting each other for hours), set the gate low (M 3–4), and let the whole thing detonate in three beats. Pacing tools are dials, not laws. (The `sex` skill's type table — quickie, angry, reunion, etc. — is the map for when to burn fast.)

## The Four States

The meters' independence creates four zones. Each has a distinct texture, a distinct use, and distinct prose.

### P high, M low — the body ahead of the mind
The body responds; the mind is absent, elsewhere, or refusing. Reads as *hollow, mechanical, going through the motions* — or as a body betraying a reluctant mind.
**Uses:** the character doing this to feel something and feeling nothing; grief-sex and detachment arcs; a failing relationship's fade-to-mechanical; the deliberate hollow climax. In dark or reluctant scenes, this state demands care — the prose must keep the character's true state legible (see The Orgasm Gate, above).
**To fix or play it:** raise M with meaning, or *play* the emptiness as the beat. Do not let it sit unnamed.

### M high, P low — the mind on fire
Desire is enormous; the body has barely started. Reads as *the tease, the almost, unbearable wanting* — the most erotically charged state in fiction.
**Uses:** seduction arcs, clothed tension, denial and edging, first-kiss scenes, everything in `foreplay` and `seduction`. This is the state readers call "the tension."
**To fix or play it:** don't touch where it wants — give the mind what it wants (words, sight, proximity, the promise) until the body catches up. Or hold it: an M-9/P-4 character can sustain an entire scene of near-touches.

### Both high — the point of no return
Both meters at the ceiling. Reads as *inevitable*. The gate is open or about to open.
**Uses:** the chosen moment — this is where the approach to orgasm lives (see the `climax` skill: stretch the approach, keep the peak brief).
**To fix or play it:** if this is your moment, write it. If it's arrived early, *don't* fake a brake with interruption — move the goalpost (she stops him: "not here," "I want to look at you first"), which drops P a point or two and raises M, buying the scene you wanted.

### Both low — not yet a scene
Neither meter engaged. Reads as *cold, pre-story*.
**Uses:** the beginning of a slow burn, or a deliberate chill (the character who isn't into it, the wrong person, the moment before the decision).
**To fix or play it:** this is `seduction` and `foreplay` territory — build M first (attention, words, the gaze), and let P follow the mind. Trying to raise P first (sudden groping) from a cold state is the fastest way to write an unconvincing scene.

## Running the System in a Scene

### The start read
Before the scene's first touch, set both meters for *each* character. A scene never starts at 0/0 unless its point is the slowest of slow burns — and it rarely starts at 8/8 unless its point is speed. Where you start is a statement: two characters who come into the room at M 6 (history, unresolved tension, a text earlier) can reach a *doorway* scene at full heat; two strangers at M 2 need a scene of courtship before the first kiss earns anything.

### The checkpoint rhythm
You don't track continuously — you check at transitions, after every two or three beats, off-page:

1. **Where are P and M now, for each character?**
2. **Is anyone near the gate early?** → plateau P, move the goalpost, or choose the climax.
3. **Is anyone's P running more than ~3 ahead of their M?** → leash rule: lift M or drop P.
4. **What is the next beat's job?** — raise P, raise M, hold both, or cool? Pick beats that do the job you need, not the job that's easiest to write.
5. **When the gate opens, is it the scene's chosen moment?** — if yes, write the approach; if no, move the goalpost before the threshold.

### Engine settings (characterize the meters)
The meters should *differ per character*, and the differences are characterization:
- **Edge distance** — how close to 10 this character runs before cresting (some tip at 9; others hold 10 for minutes).
- **M-dependence** — whether this character can crest with a flat mind at all (many can't; arousal non-concordance is real — body and mind genuinely run on separate rails, and for some characters the mind is *required*). A character who can't come without M engagement is a different person on the page than one who comes easily and hates it.
- **Sensitivity map** — which acts move P fastest *for them* (a neck-driven character and a clit-driven character need entirely different rate tables).
- **Refractory curve** — how the meters reset after orgasm. Typically P resets low; M varies wildly: it can stay high (round two, multiples), or dump hard — the male "sudden clear head" is a real and usable beat, the wanting that evaporates the instant it's satisfied, and the guilt or tenderness that rushes in behind it.
- **Tell vocabulary** — each character's outward signs at each band (see the [tracking sheet](references/tracking_sheet.md) for full tables).

### After the gate
Orgasm resets the *body*, not the *scene*. P drops (softly for most, hard for male-character refractory), while M may persist, climb, or transform into the afterglow — the emotional landing that belongs to the `aftercare` skill, and the second wind that belongs to multiples. The system's reset rule: **after a climax, re-read both meters before writing the next beat.** The character who just came and is already reaching for the other again is running on M; the one who just came and goes quiet is running on the comedown. Write the meter that's actually there.

## Tells & Writing

The meters are scaffolding; the prose is the building. Show the numbers through what the body and mind *do* at each level — see the [tracking sheet reference](references/tracking_sheet.md) for the full tell tables (body signals, interior signals, and speech register per band). The core principles:

- **Never announce the meters.** "Her arousal climbed to an eight" is the system leaking. Write the flush, the breath, the thought that won't stop circling.
- **Different meters, different prose.** High M is *interior* — thoughts, fragments, the narrowing of attention, what she notices and can't stop noticing. High P is *physical* — breath, muscle, sound, the body acting ahead of intention. A scene that reads same-y is usually a scene running on only one meter.
- **Match prose rhythm to the state.** M-high moments can slow the prose to a crawl (the held look, the long sentence, the interior digression). P-high moments tighten it — short sentences, the body driving the verbs.
- **Two characters, two sets of meters.** The most alive scenes run mismatched — one ready, one not; one cresting while the other watches; one begging while the other decides. Check both characters at every checkpoint, not just the POV one. (In RP, this is the whole game — see the tracking sheet for a shared format.)
- **"Close" is a promise.** "I'm close" means the gate is opening. Spoken and unfulfilled, it becomes a lie and the reader stops believing the meters; spoken and *interrupted* ("I'm close —" and then the stop), it's a goalpost move and lands beautifully.

## Common Pitfalls

1. **The fuse ladder.** Pure physical escalation, act chained to act, no M-beats between. The scene ends in five paragraphs or dies of a doorbell. → Interleave.
2. **Narrating the meters.** Characters who report their arousal level. Keep the scaffolding off-page (except as deliberate, charged dirty talk — "you're so close" is spoken *because* it's hot, which is different).
3. **Contrived interruptions.** The phone, the knock, the "suddenly she remembered the oven." If you must brake, brake with a *choice* — the character's own "not yet," not the universe's.
4. **The endless plateau.** Holding at P 9 for three pages of the *same* act reads static, not tense. Plateaus need movement inside them: dialogue, power shifts, new acts within the held level, or the goalpost moving up.
5. **One meter per scene.** The scene that only raises M (talk, talk, talk, nothing ever happens) is as flat as the scene that only raises P. The system's whole point is the *alternation*.
6. **Tracking only the POV character.** The other body is running its own meters — and mismatch is where tension, comedy, and tenderness live. Track both.
7. **Gate-broken climaxes with no visible emptiness.** If you climax a character hollow, show the hollowness. Otherwise the reader assumes satisfaction and the aftermath reads wrong.
8. **Uniform engines.** Every character with the same sensitivity, same M-dependence, same refractory. Differentiate the meters and the scenes differentiate themselves.
9. **Using the system on scenes that want speed.** Quickies, angry sex, raw reunion — fast-burn mode is a setting, not a failure. Don't slow a scene whose point is detonation.

## Quick Reference: State → Move

| State | What's happening | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| P near 10, M below gate | Body ready, mind not | Lift M (words, meaning, vulnerability) — or deliver a *deliberately* hollow crest and show it — or plateau P with a zone switch |
| P near 10, M at gate | The chosen moment | Write the approach (`climax` skill). Or, if early: goalpost move before the gate opens |
| M high, P low | The tease | Feed the mind (words, sight, proximity, the promise) until P catches up; hold as long as you like |
| P > M by 3+ | Body outrunning heart | Leash rule: lift M or drop P — unless hollowness is the point, then make it visible |
| Both mid | The body of the scene | Alternate dials; decide which meter the next beat builds |
| Both low | Pre-scene | `seduction` / `foreplay` territory — build M first |
| Post-orgasm | Meters reset | Re-read both meters before the next beat; the afterglow runs on whatever M survived |

## Worked Example: Annotated Run

Two characters — R and S — old almost-lovers, alone at a party after months apart. Scene intent: first time together, slow, emotionally charged. Gate set at M 9 (meaning-heavy climax, late in the scene).

| # | Beat | R: P / M | S: P / M | System move |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The look across the room; he crosses to her. Their history is in the air. | 2 / 7 | 2 / 7 | **Start read:** mind-lit, body-cold. Both meters identical — mirroring says *inevitable*; low P says *no rush*. |
| 2 | "Come outside with me." Hand at the small of her back through the dress. | 4 / 8 | 4 / 8 | P rises a little; the *hand at the back* is M. |
| 3 | Outside. Cold air. He puts his coat on her shoulders — a small act of care. | 4 / 9 | 5 / 8 | **Meaning-bomb (small):** M spikes; cold air holds P down naturally. |
| 4 | The kiss — long, deep, bodies pressed against the wall. | 7 / 9 | 7 / 9 | P climbs to 7. M near gate already. Both fine — the leash holds. |
| 5 | He pulls back, forehead to hers, breathing hard. "Tell me you want this." | 7 / 10 | 7 / 10 | **Goalpost move (his):** the pause drops nothing and raises everything — the *ask* makes it mean more. M hits ceiling; P plateaus on the held breath. |
| 6 | She answers by taking his hand and pulling him to the car. | 8 / 10 | 8 / 10 | Destination redefined: *home*. Tension relocates. |
| 7 | The drive: silence, his hand on her thigh, the dark. | 7 / 10 | 7 / 10 | Travel cools P a notch, keeps M at ceiling. The *waiting* is the scene. |
| 8 | Door. Clothes half-off in the hall. His mouth on her neck, her hands at his belt. | 9 / 10 | 9 / 10 | P climbing into plateau territory. Both near the gate — but the scene isn't done. |
| 9 | She pushes him back against the wall, stills his hands. "Not yet. I want to look at you." | 9 / 10 | 8 / 10 | **Goalpost move (hers):** she seizes the pacing. His P drops; her gaze-undoing of him *is* the next M-beat for both. Power passes. |
| 10 | She undresses him slowly, watching, while he watches her watch. | 9 / 10 | 8→9 / 10 | The plateau, worked from inside: new act, held level, power shifting back and forth. |
| 11 | The writer's chosen moment arrives — mouth and hands together, her on the edge with him — and the gate opens. | 10 / 10 | 10 / 10 | Point of no return. Write the approach, then the peak (`climax`), then the re-read of both meters in the aftermath (`aftercare`). |

**What the system did here:** the physical fuse was never slowed — it ran nearly the whole scene at 7–9. What stretched the scene was the *goalpost moves* (the ask, the car, "not yet"), each one a character choice that cooled P and raised M, relocating the tension instead of killing it. Every time the scene threatened to end, a character redefined what "enough" meant.
